How it works

Every pull request gets its own production-like environment.

Branchglass connects to your repository and your cloud account, then provisions an isolated, fully configured copy of your stack the moment a PR opens — real services, real migrations, safe synthetic data. Reviewers get a shareable URL instead of a five-page local setup guide, and the whole thing deletes itself when the branch lands.

15-minute setup, no agents to install Runs inside your own AWS, GCP or Azure account Zero orphaned infrastructure after merge
  1. 1 Connect Repository + cloud role once per repo
  2. 2 Pull request opens Webhook fires on the new branch 1.8s to detect
  3. 3 Environment builds Prod config, synthetic data 41s median
  4. 4 Test & review One shareable URL for everyone updates on push
  5. 5 Merge or close Everything is destroyed 6s teardown

Questions teams ask

Before you connect a repo

Does Branchglass run in our cloud or yours?
In yours. Branchglass deploys into a role you create in your own AWS, GCP or Azure account, inside a sandbox boundary with a hard spend cap. We never copy your production data out of your account, and you can revoke the role at any time from your own console.
What data ends up in a preview environment?
Synthetic data, generated from your schema and column statistics. Customer records, payment details and anything tagged as PII are never copied. A typical environment seeds 18,204 rows — roughly 25% of production volume — which is enough to reproduce realistic list, pagination and edge-case behaviour.
How long does the initial setup take?
Around 15 minutes for the first repository: one OAuth grant, one cloud role, and we generate a .branchglass.yml from what we find in your infrastructure. The median team that finished onboarding on 14 March had its first preview environment running in 14m 20s. Managed Kubernetes and multi-cloud setups usually take a bit longer.
What happens when someone pushes a new commit mid-review?
The environment updates in place. Branchglass only rebuilds the services touched by the diff, so an incremental update averages 22 seconds instead of a full 41-second provision — and the same URL keeps working, so reviewers never lose their place. Test data is preserved unless the migration changes the schema, in which case we re-seed and post a note on the PR.
How do you keep costs predictable?
Every environment has an hourly budget cap and pauses itself after 45 minutes idle. A preview lives for a maximum of 7 days, and is destroyed within seconds of a merge or close. Each pull request gets a cost summary posted back to the thread — this example came in at $0.94.
