The program

Four things you can only get in the room

Stackglass Live is built around material that does not survive a slide deck. Twelve program slots between 8:00 AM and 6:30 PM, one hallway track, and a rooftop to argue about it afterwards.

Practitioner talks

Nine talks from people who own production systems: internal platforms, reliability, infrastructure and developer experience. Submissions were reviewed against one bar — did you actually run this, and what did it cost you?

Questions

The things people email us about

Still stuck? register@stackglass.live is answered by a human, usually within a few hours.

Can I transfer my pass or get a refund?

Yes. Full refunds are available until Friday, September 17, 2027 — email register@stackglass.live with your order number and we process it within three business days. After that date, passes are transferable to a colleague free of charge until Friday, October 8, 2027, and we will reissue the badge and dietary details. No-shows can roll their pass forward to Stackglass Live 2028 at no extra cost.

Do I have to choose sessions in advance?

No. Both stages and every talk are covered by one badge, and you can walk between Main Stage and Studio B whenever you like. Two things do need a pick: the 60 simulation seats (choose Wave A or Wave B when you register) and the six office-hours tables (choose two priorities). You can change either until Friday, October 8, 2027 by editing your confirmation link, and remaining seats are released on site at the coordinator desk.

What is the live architecture teardown, exactly?

It is a 60-minute, unedited walk through the evolution of a real production system — this year, Northwind Pay's payments ledger, from a single monolith to 40 services as the team grew from 14 to 90 engineers. The speakers bring the actual dashboards, the postmortems for three of its worst incidents, and the design decisions they would reverse. The last 20 minutes are audience questions, and yes, the hard ones get answered.

How does the incident-response simulation work?

Ninety minutes, teams of six, two parallel waves of 30 seats each in Sandbox Rooms A and B. You are handed a sandboxed environment with live dashboards and an incident that gets worse on a schedule. Facilitators score detection, communication and follow-through, then run a joint debrief. Laptops are optional — a web terminal is provided — and solo attendees are placed on a team. No Kubernetes experience is assumed, and the room is cell-free and unrecorded.

Is lunch included, and what about dietary needs?

Lunch, two coffee breaks and the rooftop gathering are all included in every pass, including the Student & Nonprofit pass. The lunch buffet has labelled vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, halal and nut-free options, plus a dedicated nut-free station. Add your requirements during registration and the catering team prints them on your badge — if something looks wrong on the day, ask any crew member and they will sort it.

How many people attend, and is it worth it for a small team?

The room is capped at 500 and 412 passes were claimed as of this morning. Roughly 38% of attendees are platform and infrastructure engineers and 24% are SREs, so most of the hallway track is your peer group. Two-or-three-person teams are common; the Team Pass covers five and includes a private 25-minute office-hours table, which is usually the highest-value hour of the day for a team that arrives with a shared problem.

Will the talks be recorded, and what do I need to bring?

Main Stage sessions are filmed and available to attendees for 30 days after the event; Studio B talks are released only where the speaker agrees. The simulation is never recorded. Bring layers for the rooftop, a charger, and a laptop only if you prefer your own keyboard for the simulation — everything else, including notebooks, water and Wi-Fi, is provided.

Can my company sponsor or send a bigger group?

Team Passes cover five seats each and stack cleanly — a 12-person group is two Team Passes plus two Standard Passes. For groups above 20, or for a sponsor table in the atrium, email partners@stackglass.live. Sponsors get a table, a logo on the attendee wall and the community raffle, but no stage time: the program is set by the review committee and that is not for sale.
