Flagship session · 1:00 – 2:05 PM · Main Hall

The eval loved it. Production didn't.

A live failure-analysis session in which two engineering teams open their own incident timelines in public. No best-practice slides — production dashboards, the fixing commits, and an honest accounting of what the evaluation suite never saw.

Case 1 · Cartogra Payments

Chargeback triage assistant

Shipped June 2027 to 340 support agents. Passed a 400-query golden set with a 94% pass rate. Ten days later it was routing 7.8% of tickets to a human — up from 1.2% — and finance noticed before engineering did.

“Bring your worst eval. We'll show you the four questions that would have caught this in one afternoon.”

Marcus Bell · Director of Reliability, Sentry Grid

What actually broke — four findings
  • The eval snapshot was 41 days old. The index rebuilds nightly; the frozen snapshot still contained a shard that had been dropped from production in July.
  • The chunker silently discarded tables. 100% of the refund policy lived in tables, so the model answered policy questions from memory.
  • A 30-second tool timeout met a 9-second client timeout. Retries tripled peak load and pushed p95 from 840 ms to 2.41 s.
  • The golden set was written by the team that built the feature. 400 queries, all phrased the way the builders phrase things.
Case 2 · Kestrel Cloud · incident summarizer

On-call engineers spent a median of 14 minutes reading summaries during the pilot and 26 minutes during their first real Sev-1. Summary usefulness, rated by the same eleven engineers, fell from 91% to 74% in eleven days. Cause: the prompt included the last 30 minutes of the incident channel — which contains the mitigation.

Questions

Before you register

Still unsure? Email hello@modelcraftsummit.com and a human replies within one business day, usually with more detail than you asked for.

What exactly does the $545 ticket include?
Everything on the day: all 11 talks and breakouts, your choice of one hands-on evaluation lab, a sit-down lunch with three hot mains, morning and afternoon coffee, the evening gathering (food plus two drinks), office-hours access, the printed field guide, and 30-day access to every session recording. There is no extra charge for the labs and no upsell booth anywhere in the venue.
Can I transfer my ticket or get a refund?
Yes to both, with dates attached:
  • Until September 1, 2027: full refund minus a $35 processing fee.
  • Until October 1, 2027: free transfer to any colleague — send us their name and email and we reissue the badge and lab seat.
  • After October 1, 2027: transfers only, and lab seats move to the waitlist instead of transferring.
Is there a virtual or livestream option?
No. Modelcraft Summit is in-person only — 450 seats, one room, and the failure analysis depends on a room full of people who will actually ask hard questions. Every attendee gets 30-day access to the recordings, and four sessions are published free in November 2027.
Who else is going to be there?

Typical titles on the badge list include staff engineer, applied scientist, head of platform, and reliability lead.
Do I need to bring anything to the lab?
A laptop is optional — 42 lab machines are set up with everything preloaded. If you'd like your own environment, bring a laptop running Node 20 or Python 3.11 or newer. All datasets are local and anonymized: no cloud account, no API key, no network access required during the session. Notebooks and data are published to attendees 48 hours after the summit.
