Incident-response simulation · Simulation Lab

Practice a SEV-2 before you have to handle a real one

You'll work a realistic incident from the first page to the resolution, in a room of people doing the same. No one's real systems are involved, but the incident is designed to feel real.

  1. Form a team

    Six people per team, mixed across companies. The roles are incident commander, ops lead, comms lead, scribe and two responders.

  2. Take the page

    A SEV-2 lands in a simulated production environment with dashboards, logs, deploy history and a noisy chat channel.

  3. Work it live

    Facilitators add new signals as you go: a misleading alert, an executive asking for an ETA and a support queue that keeps filling up.

  4. Debrief

    Tomás Reyes and experienced incident commanders run a 20-minute review. Each team leaves with its own incident timeline.

Deciding whether to come

Is it worth giving up a Saturday?

This is a quick guide to whether the day suits you, so you don't spend money on the wrong conference.

It's a good fit if you…

  • build or run an internal platform, developer portal, CI/CD system or shared infrastructure
  • carry a pager, run incident reviews or own SLOs for services other teams depend on
  • lead a platform, SRE, infrastructure or developer experience team, or are about to
  • want detailed, honest architecture stories and time to ask your own questions

It's probably not the right fit if you…

  • need to attend remotely (nothing is streamed live)
  • want vendor demos or an expo floor (there isn't one)
  • haven't operated software in production yet (sessions assume on-call or infrastructure experience)

FAQ

Questions people ask before registering

Still deciding?

Email hello@stackglass.live and a member of the organizing team will reply, usually within one business day.

What does a ticket include?

Every ticket covers all sessions: the talks, the live architecture teardown, one incident-response simulation round (while seats last) and office hours sign-up. It also covers lunch and the evening gathering on the Fulton Works rooftop. There are no paid add-ons.

Is there a virtual or streaming option?

No, Stackglass Live is in person only. Main Hall talks are recorded and shared with registered attendees within 14 days. The teardown and the simulation aren't recorded so that presenters and participants can speak openly.

Do I need a laptop for the simulation?

Yes. Any laptop with a current version of Chrome, Firefox, Safari or Edge will work. Everything runs in the browser, so you don't install anything or connect to your own systems. Every team table has a power strip.

How do office hours work?

On October 1, 2027, registered attendees get an email with a link to choose up to two 25-minute sessions. Each group has at most six people. Seats that are still open on the day are released at the Salons desk at 1:00 PM.

What's the refund and transfer policy?

You can get a full refund through September 30, 2027. After that, tickets aren't refundable, but you can transfer yours to a colleague for free until October 14, 2027. Use the link in your confirmation email.

Can my company pay by invoice?

Yes, for teams of five or more. Choose the team rate, then select "Pay by invoice" in your confirmation email. We accept purchase orders and net-30 terms.

Is the venue accessible?

Yes. Fulton Works has step-free entry, elevators to every floor including the rooftop, gender-neutral restrooms, a quiet room and a lactation room. Main Hall sessions have live captions. You can tell us about any other needs when you register.

Is there a code of conduct?

Yes. Everyone at the event, including attendees, speakers and staff, agrees to the Stackglass Live code of conduct. Trained staff are at the registration desk all day, and you can also text them at (312) 555-0164.
