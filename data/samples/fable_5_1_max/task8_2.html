How it works

From first event to production-ready handler — without touching production.

Point a provider at a temporary endpoint, watch what it really sends, and iterate until your handler survives everything you can throw at it.

  1. 01

    Spin up a temporary endpoint

    Create a unique HTTPS URL in one click and paste it into any provider's webhook settings. No deploys, no tunnels, no edits to your production config.

    https://in.patchbay.cloud/e/7f3k2a
  2. 02

    Capture and inspect everything

    Every delivery is stored with full headers, the raw body, timing and a signature check — so you see what the provider actually sends, not what the docs say it sends.

  3. 03

    Replay, rewrite and break it

    Re-send captured events to a local or staging service, edit payloads to cover edge cases, and simulate timeouts, duplicates and bad signatures on demand.

  4. 04

    Ship the handler, share the trail

    Generate a handler snippet for the exact event types you captured, with verification wired in. Share the session so support sees what engineering saw.

Features

Everything between “the provider sent it” and “we handled it correctly.”

Webhook bugs hide in the gap between documentation and reality. Patchbay Cloud puts the real events in front of you and gives you the tools to poke at them.

Temporary endpoints

Disposable HTTPS URLs that receive and store every webhook delivery. Create one per integration, per branch or per bug report, and tear it down when you're done.

Full-fidelity inspection

Headers, query string, raw and parsed body, timing and the result of signature verification — for every event, searchable by type, ID or time.

Replay & payload editing

Re-deliver any captured event to any target. Change a field, swap an ID, or send the same event ten times to prove your handler is idempotent.

Failure scenarios

Simulate timeouts, duplicate deliveries, out-of-order events and invalid signatures on demand — before a provider's retry storm does it for you.

Shared histories & sessions

Event histories and live debugging sessions belong to your workspace, so a “the webhook never arrived” ticket links straight to the evidence — for support and engineering alike.

Generated code snippets

FAQ

Questions teams ask before joining.

Does Patchbay Cloud sit in front of my production endpoints?

No. Patchbay Cloud creates temporary endpoints that you point providers at while you build and debug. Your production configuration doesn't change until you decide to switch the destination URL over — and by then you've already seen every event type and edge case the provider sends.

Which webhook providers does it work with?

Any service that delivers webhooks over HTTP or HTTPS — payment processors, source-control and CI platforms, commerce platforms, messaging APIs, or your own internal services. During the private beta, signature verification covers common signing methods such as HMAC-based header signatures and timestamped signatures. If your provider uses something unusual, tell us when you join the waitlist.

Can I replay events to my local machine?

Yes. Replay any captured event to any target URL — a staging environment, or a local server exposed through the tunnel you already use. Edit the payload first to test a variation, or replay it several times to check that your handler is idempotent.

How do shared sessions work between engineering and support?

Every temporary endpoint keeps an event history that your whole workspace can browse. Support can find the exact delivery a customer is asking about — by ID, type or time — and share a link to the event or to a live debugging session. Engineering opens the link and sees the same headers, payload, attempts and replays. No screenshots, no copy-pasted logs.

What happens to the data I capture?

Webhook payloads often contain sensitive information, so endpoints are temporary by design and their histories are scoped to your workspace. You can delete an endpoint and everything it captured at any time. We'll share full data-handling details with beta participants before their first session.

When will I get access, and what does it cost?

We're inviting teams in small batches so we can work closely with each one; joining the waitlist early and telling us what you're integrating helps us prioritize. The private beta is free for participating teams, and beta teams will hear about pricing before general availability.

What do you expect from beta participants?

Honest feedback. We'll check in every few weeks, ask what broke and what's missing, and shape the roadmap — especially signing methods and transports — around what participating teams actually need.
