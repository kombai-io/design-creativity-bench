Why attend

Built for the engineers who own AI systems after launch

If you build, ship, or get paged for AI in production, this day is organized around the problems you actually have: evaluations that don't predict real behavior, retrieval that drifts, inference that costs too much, agents that wander, and reliability work with no playbook yet.

Talks by practitioners

Sessions are led by engineers who build and operate production AI systems — on model evaluation, retrieval systems, inference, agents, and production reliability.

A failure, analyzed live

Watch a real AI application get taken apart on stage: how it behaved in testing, how it behaved in production, and what explains the gap.

Hands-on evaluation

Build and score evaluations yourself in a lab using sample model outputs and datasets. Leave with methods you can apply on Monday.

Direct access

Small-group office hours put you at the table with speakers and experienced AI engineering leaders — bring the problem you're stuck on.

Program

Practitioner talks across five areas

The program features talks from engineers working on production AI systems, grounded in what those systems do once they're live — not what the demo promised.

  1. 01

    Model evaluation

    Designing evaluations that predict production behavior, choosing metrics that reflect what users experience, and recognizing when a benchmark has stopped telling you anything useful.

  2. 02

    Retrieval systems

    Indexing, chunking, ranking, and freshness — and how retrieval quality shows up, or hides, in end-user outcomes.

  3. 03

    Inference

    Latency, throughput, and cost trade-offs when serving models: batching, caching, capacity planning, and knowing which knob to turn first.

  4. 04

    Agents

    Tool use, planning, and guardrails for systems that take actions — and how to test them before they take the wrong one.

  5. 05

    Production reliability

    Monitoring, incident response, and the failure modes specific to AI systems, from silent degradation to upstream model changes.

Live session

A live failure analysis: testing vs. production

One AI application. Two very different behaviors. This session examines how the application performed during testing, what changed once it met real users and real data, and how the gap was found and understood.

  • How the application behaved in testing — and why that looked fine at the time
  • How it behaved in production — and how the difference first showed up
  • What the gap reveals about evaluation, data, and monitoring for your own systems

Beyond the talks

Do the work, then ask the people who've done it

Two formats built for participation rather than passive listening — both included with registration.

Hands-on

Evaluation lab

Work with sample model outputs and datasets to build, run, and score evaluations. You'll compare approaches with other attendees, see where they disagree, and leave with methods you can take back to your own systems.

  1. Start with real material. Sample model outputs and datasets are provided — no setup required beyond your laptop.
  2. Define what "good" means. Turn fuzzy quality goals into checks you can actually run.
  3. Score and compare. See where your evaluations agree, where they disagree, and what they miss.

Bring a laptop

Small groups

Office hours

Small-group office hours give you direct access to speakers and experienced AI engineering leaders. Bring the problem you're stuck on — a flaky eval, a retrieval pipeline that drifts, an agent that won't stay in bounds — and work through it with people who have solved similar ones.

  • Kept deliberately small, so everyone at the table gets time to talk
  • Led by speakers and experienced AI engineering leaders
  • Come with specific questions about your own systems

FAQ

Questions before you register

Who is Modelcraft Summit for?

Engineers building and operating production AI systems: ML and software engineers, platform and infrastructure engineers, reliability engineers, and technical leads responsible for AI features that real users depend on. Talks assume you've worked on a system in production, or are about to.

What does registration include?

All sessions — the practitioner talks, the live failure-analysis session, the hands-on evaluation lab, and small-group office hours — plus lunch and the evening gathering.

Is there a virtual option?

No. Modelcraft Summit is an in-person event. All sessions take place in Chicago on Saturday, October 16, 2027.

Where in Chicago is it held?

The summit takes place in Chicago, Illinois. Registered attendees receive venue and arrival details by email.

What happens in the evaluation lab?

You'll work with sample model outputs and datasets to build and score evaluations, compare approaches with other attendees, and see where different methods agree, disagree, and miss. Bring a laptop.

How do office hours work?

Office hours run in small groups so every attendee gets direct time with speakers and experienced AI engineering leaders. Come with specific questions about your own systems — the more concrete, the better.

What should I bring?

A laptop for the hands-on evaluation lab, and a real problem or two for office hours. Everything else — including lunch and the evening gathering — is covered by your registration.
