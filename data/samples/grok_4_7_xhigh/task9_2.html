01 — Is this your room

A day for operators, not a tour of booths.

“We built the day we wanted after a bad launch: one room, no booths, and the afternoon spent on evidence.”

Amira Shah, staff engineer at Northline and summit chair

Come if

  • You own evaluation, retrieval, inference, agents, or reliability for a system people actually use.
  • Your offline scores and production behavior disagree, and you want a method rather than a pep talk.
  • You want fifty minutes with a speaker in a group of eight, not a hallway ambush.
  • You can give Saturday. Talks are single-track from 8:45 AM to 5:40 PM, with the evening in the same building.

Skip if

  • You want a vendor showcase. There is no expo floor and no paid speaking slot. Tickets fund the day.
  • You need a virtual pass. Modelcraft is in person only. Talk recordings come later; the lab and office hours are not recorded.
  • You want an introduction to models. Talks assume you have shipped or operated one against real users.
  • You want posters, a job fair, or a lead list. None of those are on the schedule.

02 — Talks

Five practitioner talks. One room.

The morning is model evaluation, retrieval systems, inference, agents, and production reliability, in that order. Thirty minutes each. No sponsor slot between them. Abstracts are below; the clock is in the schedule.

  1. 9:05 AM

    Evals that survive contact with users

    Elena Cho · Evaluation lead, Copperline · The Floor · 30 min

    Copperline’s weekly eval is a sample drawn from that week’s traces, not a frozen trophy set. Elena shows the sampling rules, the items they refuse to score, and the three numbers allowed to page someone. You leave with a one-page spec for a weekly production sample.

  2. 9:40 AM

    Retrieval under drift

    Mateo Alvarez · Retrieval systems, Fieldnote · The Floor · 30 min

    Fieldnote diffs citations, not just final answers, before a retrieval change ships. Mateo covers freshness checks, the week an index lagged a policy update, and the cheap probe that would have caught it. For people who already run hybrid search.

  3. 10:30 AM

    Inference budgets, not vibes

    Julian Okonkwo · Head of inference, Harborline · The Floor · 30 min

    Harborline treats p95 latency as a contract with a number on it. Batching, when speculative decoding pays, and a model upgrade that cost 80 milliseconds and was rolled back before lunch. No vendor bake-off.

  4. 11:05 AM

    Agents that know when to stop

    David Rhee · Agent platform, Kiln Systems · The Floor · 30 min

    Tool loops fail in expensive ways. David walks a trace where the agent was confident and wrong for nine calls, the stop rules Kiln runs now, and the handoff to a person.

  5. 11:40 AM

    Reliability contracts for model services

    Naomi Berg and Priya Venkatesh · Westbridge and Latticeform · The Floor · 30 min

    What an SLO looks like when the output is prose. Error budgets, how many traces to read, rollback, and the question they ask before a prompt change: what would make us revert by lunch?

Then the afternoon. The recording cannot replace the next three blocks: the failure analysis, the lab, and office hours.

03 — Live failure-analysis session

91 in staging, 62 on Tuesday.

1:15–2:25 PM on The Floor. Seventy minutes. Elena Cho, Mateo Alvarez, and Naomi Berg walk the traces. Amira Shah chairs. Helen Park and Luis Ortega respond from the operator side.

The case is Northwind Assist, a composite built for teaching from production failure modes. It is not a postmortem of a named employer, and you will not be asked to present your own incident. Optional: bring a sanitized trace if you want a second opinion at office hours.

What the session examines

  • The retrieval index in production lagged the eval snapshot by 11 days. Offline retrieval of the current policy was 97.2%. In production that week it was 71.0%.
  • The rubric awarded partial credit for fluent apologies. Polite non-answers looked like partial success and hid missing actions.
  • A tool schema changed on 4 March 2027. The harness still had the old schema. Tool-call accuracy fell from 88.6% offline to 64.3% on live traffic.

You leave with a one-page timeline method. Ticket holders get the recording within 10 days. The printed packet is handed out at the session door; Leadership passes receive it at check-in so they can read it at lunch.

11 — Questions

Before you spend the $695.

Who should attend?

Engineers building and operating production AI systems: evaluation, retrieval, inference, agents, and reliability. Managers who still read traces are welcome — 46 already registered. If you have not shipped or operated a model against real users, this will be the wrong altitude.

What does registration include?

Registration includes all sessions, lunch, and an evening gathering. That means the five talks, the live failure-analysis session, the evaluation lab and Packet B, office-hour signup, coffee from 7:45 AM, lunch at The Long Table, and the Kiln Room gathering from 6:15 to 8:30 PM with two drinks. Recordings of the talks and the failure session go to ticket holders by October 26. The lab and office hours are not recorded.

Is the lab an extra ticket?

No. The lab is included. You get a printed packet and a sealed slice of 20 outputs either way. A powered bench is separate only in the sense that 80 of them exist and 22 remain. Request one on the form. If the benches fill, you still have a hall seat, the same packet, and a roaming facilitator.

Will I actually get an office hour?

Not automatically. 64 seats, 53 claimed, 11 open as of 13 September. Leadership ($890) reserves one; if we cannot seat your three ranked tables we move you to Practitioner and refund the $195 difference. Practitioner and Team can request a seat. Helen Park and Luis Ortega run unreserved overflow at the Atrium window during the same hour. You do not miss a talk either way — office hours do not overlap the program.

Is there a virtual option?

No. October 16 is in person only. Talks and the failure analysis are recorded for people who bought a ticket, not sold as a stream. If you cannot be in Chicago that Saturday, this is not your event.

What is the refund and transfer policy?

Full refunds ended Saturday 4 September 2027. Through Saturday 25 September you can cancel for 50%. After that, tickets are not refunded. You can transfer a badge to a colleague through Saturday 9 October. Write desk@modelcraftsummit.com with the reference number.

What should I bring, and what about access?

A photo ID that matches the badge name, and a laptop if you want to score Packet B in the file rather than on paper. The hall runs cool. Dress is work clothes. Step-free entry is on Morgan Street. Captioning is on for talks. For anything else — a quiet-room plan, parking, step-free detail — write access@modelcraftsummit.com by Friday 2 October, or use the notes on the form. We have already arranged accommodations for 14 registrants.

Can my employer pay? Is there a student rate?

Yes to the first. The confirmation includes a short description you can forward, and the desk issues one reference per team order. There is no student rate. If the standard price is the only thing between you and the room, write the desk by Monday 20 September. We have helped 9 attendees that way and we answer either yes or no.

What should I not expect?

No expo, no booth scans, no lead-list export, no job fair, no introductory tutorial, no virtual seat, and no open bar past the two drinks on the badge. Breakfast is coffee and morning buns, not a plated meal. We will not ask you to disclose a confidential incident.
