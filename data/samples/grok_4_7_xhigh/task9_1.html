01

The day

Decide if this is your room.

Stackglass Live is a one-day conference for people who build internal platforms and keep production systems reliable. Registration is the whole day: every session, lunch in Fulton Hall, and the evening gathering. There is no expo hall and no virtual ticket.

Come if

  • You run an internal platform, a reliability practice, infrastructure, or a developer-experience program.
  • You want a real production system examined in public, including the seams that aged badly.
  • You can be in Chicago on Saturday, October 16, and you want office hours that are scheduled, not a hallway lottery.

Skip if

  • You want a vendor floor or a recruiting hall. There is not one.
  • You want an introductory Kubernetes course or a certification lab. Talks assume you already operate production systems.
  • You cannot attend in person that Saturday. Sessions are not streamed, and there is no Sunday repeat.

03

Architecture teardown

One system, four epochs.

The teardown is the center of the day. Ledgerline is a real payments company. Jordan Okonkwo, distinguished engineer there, will show how the production system changed as both usage and the engineering organization grew. Maya Chen sits in; she consulted on their internal platform in 2025 and will interrupt when the diagram and the org chart disagree.

The diagram we were afraid to publish in 2022, and the one we run now. Jordan Okonkwo, Distinguished Engineer, Ledgerline
  1. 2019 Checkout monolith 12 engineers. One region. One deploy on Fridays, if the week had been kind.
  2. 2021 Ledger service Extracted ledger. 40 engineers. Still one primary region, and a queue that hid a bad week.
  3. 2024 Control plane Multi-region control plane. 180 engineers. A failover that mostly worked, and a paved path almost nobody used.
  4. 2027 Paved path About 400 engineers. 61 product teams on the path. This is the diagram they will leave on the board.

You leave with the handout: four epochs, the seams that hurt, and which platform boundaries they would not draw again. Slides are not the point. If you only have patience for one session, make it this one. It is not recorded for the public; registered attendees receive the Assembly Hall cut within three weeks.

04

Studios

Simulation, then eight small tables.

Hands-on · 1:20–3:00 p.m.

Region stall on a Friday

Naomi Brooks, who commanded Harborgrid’s 2025 region failover, runs a 100-minute incident-response simulation. A health check goes red in one region during a Friday peak. Your group of six gets a partial runbook, a customer note, a role card, and a shared paper timeline.

You name an incident commander, decide what to tell customers, and decide what not to change. Facilitators score coordination, not heroics. No production system is connected. No exploit walkthrough, no prize for theatrics.

108 of 120 simulation places held. 12 remain. If it fills, your talks, lunch, office hours, and evening gathering still stand. Waitlist is in registration order.

  • Teams are assigned at badge pickup. You will not sit only with coworkers.
  • Workbook is printed from the registration list. A laptop helps and is not required.
  • Studios are no-photo. The debrief stays in the room.

Questions

Before you hold a seat.

Is there a livestream or a recording I can buy later?

No livestream, no single-session ticket, no public recording. Assembly Hall talks are recorded for registered attendees and released within three weeks. The simulation and office hours are not recorded.

What does registration include?

All sessions, plated lunch, coffee from 8:00 a.m., coat check, the printed program, the simulation workbook if you have a place, and the evening gathering until 8:30 p.m. Hotel and travel are not included.

What if the simulation is full when I pay?

You still have talks, lunch, office hours, and the evening. Simulation places are 108 of 120 held as of July 14. The waitlist follows payment time, not the moment you opened this page. Unpaid holds do not keep a simulation place.

How do refunds and transfers work?

Full refund through August 28, 2027. Fifty percent through September 18. None after that. A name transfer is free once, through September 18, if you email register@stackglass.live with the reference number. After that the badge name is the badge name.

What are the conduct and photo rules?

No harassment, including on the terrace. Facilitators can remove someone from a studio. You may photograph the Assembly Hall. Do not photograph the simulation, office hours, or someone else’s badge. Stackglass photographs the hall and the terrace; tell the desk if you need to be kept out of those frames.

What should I wear and bring?

Come as you work. October terraces in Chicago are cool; coat check is included. Bring a government photo ID that matches the registration. A laptop is optional. Paper is provided for the simulation timeline.

Can my four teammates stay together all day?

At lunch, yes, if you request a shared table. In the simulation, no. Teams of six are mixed at badge pickup on purpose. Office-hour chairs are individual, except the one reserved chair on a leadership pass.

When can I realistically fly home?

The program ends at 6:00 p.m. The gathering ends at 8:30 p.m. A Sunday morning flight is the realistic plan. A Saturday night flight after 9:30 p.m. is possible if you skip the terrace or leave it early. Nothing runs Sunday.
