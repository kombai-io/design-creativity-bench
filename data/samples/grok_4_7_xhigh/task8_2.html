What the bay actually does

A temporary endpoint, a shared tape of every delivery, and a way to break it on purpose.

Patchbay is not a public request bin. It is a team debugger for the awkward middle of an integration: after the provider sends a signed POST, before that handler is allowed anywhere near production data.

01 · Endpoints

Spin up a listener you can throw away.

Each bay is an HTTPS endpoint with its own signing secrets, retention clock, and allowlist. Point Stripe, GitHub, Shopify, Slack, or your own sender at it. When the investigation ends, archive the bay — the URL stops accepting traffic in under a minute.

02 · Inspect

Headers, body, and the signature math.

Every delivery is stored with raw body bytes, parsed JSON or form fields, and the verification result. Mismatch, clock skew, and missing headers are called out before anyone argues about whose bug it is.

03 · Replay and failure drills

Send it again. Then send it wrong.

Replay the original bytes, or edit a field and keep the same event id to test idempotency. Force a 500, stall the response, or strip the signature so you can watch the provider’s retry schedule without inventing a second staging account.

Questions teams ask before they apply

Straight answers.

How long does an endpoint live?

Default archive date is 14 days, editable per bay. pb_live_8k2mq4 is set to expire April 2, 2026 at 17:00 UTC. Archiving stops ingress within a minute. Captured events remain readable until the retention clock ends.

Who can see captured payloads?

Workspace members you invite, plus anyone holding a session link you explicitly enable. Support links use the redacted view. Signing secrets and Authorization headers are vaulted. The March 18 audit log shows four viewers on bay-1842.

Is the private beta free?

Yes through cohort 4. Limits are 3 active endpoints, 5 seats, and 50k deliveries per month. We will publish paid plans before anyone is asked to migrate. Preview workspaces will get 30 days’ notice.

Do you train models on our events?

No. Captures are used to render your console, run replays you trigger, and draft snippets you request. They are encrypted at rest, stored in us-west-2, and deleted at the end of retention.

What happens when we outgrow the beta?

You keep the same endpoint hostnames for the life of the bay, export the event history as JSON, and cut your provider configuration over to production on your own schedule. Patchbay never becomes the production receiver unless you leave it that way.
