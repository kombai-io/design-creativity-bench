How it works One locked master. Every market’s cut.

SceneLilt is the localization bay for marketing teams. You upload a master video and select the markets and languages. The platform transcribes the original dialogue, identifies spoken and on-screen content, translates the copy, generates dubbed audio, and adapts on-screen text.

Teams compare each localized version with the original and edit language, timing, pronunciation, and tone. Reviewers comment on and approve individual market versions. Approved videos export in the formats required for publishing. The locked picture and music bed stay put — SceneLilt does not reopen the edit.

Between picture lock and the publish sheet.

01 Ingest

Upload the master. Name the markets and languages.

The picture stays locked. A producer uploads the master and ticks the markets on the media plan — the actual flight, not a guess. SceneLilt opens one localization job, pins the brand glossary and claim rules to it, and treats that file as the source of truth for every version that follows.

Safe-area presets travel with the file, so a 9:16 social cut and a 16:9 broadcast master are planned at ingest, not invented at export.

02 Read

Transcribe the dialogue. Identify every on-screen line.

Before anyone translates, SceneLilt listens and looks. Spoken lines are transcribed to timecode with the speaker labeled. On-screen content — supers, lower thirds, pack shots, end cards, legal lines — is detected separately, so a claim is never folded into the voiceover script.

Music is marked and left alone. Only dialogue and type move downstream.

Your teamConfirms speaker labels and which on-screen lines are substantiated claims.
SceneLiltTranscribes spoken dialogue and identifies on-screen content, both locked to timecode.

03 Produce

Translate, dub, and adapt the on-screen text.

Each market gets three tracks, not one blob of “the translation.” Copy is translated against the glossary. A matched voice generates the dubbed audio. On-screen text is rewritten for length, script, and reading direction — Arabic mirrors the layout, Japanese can run vertical, German drops a word so the claim still clears the bottle.

The spoken line can keep a figure the bottle cannot fit. The footnote still travels with the claim in every language.

SceneLiltTranslates, generates dubbed audio, and adapts on-screen text per market, including right-to-left.

04 Refine

Compare with the original. Edit language, timing, pronunciation, and tone.

Put the master next to any localized version and play them on the same timecode. Nudge a line by frames if the dub lands late. Correct a pronunciation once — “Lumière” stays French in every market — and tag the tone the read should hold. Those notes feed the next pass. They do not live in a side email.

05 Review

Comment on a market. Approve that market. Leave the others moving.

Approval is per market, not per campaign. France can be in notes while Mexico is cleared for export. Comments pin to a timecode or to a specific on-screen element, and a version cannot be exported until its reviewer approves it.

Review links are scoped. Amélie Roux’s link opens only France. She cannot approve Mexico, and she never sees the edit project.

Your reviewersComment and approve their own market version. Brand can reply in the same thread.
SceneLiltKeeps the thread, the approval state, and the export hold until that market is cleared.

Export approved videos in the formats publishing actually needs.

When a market is approved, it leaves as the specs on the media plan — not a single generic download. Broadcast master, social cutdowns, burned-in or clean picture, caption sidecars. Held markets stay held, even if a preview render exists for reviewers.
