02 — Why the ecosystem matters

A connector is useful only if the objects agree.

Eighty-six names would not help a controller. A shared object model does. Every connector writes vendor, entity, GL account, cost center, cash account, and worker — or it opens an exception instead of guessing a match.

  1. 01

    Vendor

    The party you pay or collect from. One record, even when the sources use different names.

    NetSuite vendor · Stripe customer · Ramp merchant

  2. 02

    Entity

    The company, subsidiary, or brand that owns the transaction. Multi-entity stays multi-entity.

    NetSuite subsidiary · Sage location · Workday company

  3. 03

    GL account

    Where it posts. The book of record keeps the official number; LedgerNorth carries the map.

    Intacct ACCOUNTNO · QuickBooks AcctNum · Xero AccountCode

  4. 04

    Cost center

    Who owns the spend. Card programs inherit the center from workforce, not from a month-end queue.

    Workday cost center · Ramp department · QuickBooks class

  5. 05

    Cash account

    Where the money sits. Balances, payouts, and expected fees land on the same account.

    Plaid account · Mercury account · Stripe balance

  6. 06

    Worker

    Who spent or was paid. Payroll journals and cardholders resolve to the same person.

    Workday worker · Ramp cardholder · ADP employee

No silent matches. If Stripe and NetSuite disagree on a vendor, both source values stay on the exception until a person chooses.
