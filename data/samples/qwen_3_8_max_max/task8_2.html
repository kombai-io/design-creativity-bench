01 — THE WORKFLOW

      
From first event to shipped handler,without touching production.

      
Five moves, one capture session. Everything happens on a throwaway endpoint that you control end to end.

    

    

      

        
01Catch everything

        
02Inspect & replay

        
03Mutate & break it

        
04Share the session

        
05Ship the handler

        
TIP · Point your provider's test mode at the patchbay URL first. Promote to production only when the timeline is boring.

STATUS · LISTENING on wt-4f8a2c

      

      

        

          
STEP 01

          
Catch everything

          
Spin up a temporary endpoint in one click — a secret URL that accepts any method, any header, any body. No servers, no tunnels, no port forwarding. Set a TTL or keep it for the whole sprint.

          

            $ patchbay create --ttl 24h

            ✔ endpoint ready → https://pbay.dev/t/wt-4f8a2c

            ↳ accepts: POST · PUT · PATCH · any body · HMAC or plain
          

        

        

          
STEP 02

          
Inspect & replay

          
Every event lands in a queryable history with the raw body, headers and millisecond-level timing. Fire any of them back at your handler with one click — or re-deliver a whole batch with jitter, exactly like a flaky provider would.

          

            

              #431202payment_intent.succeeded
              ⟲ REPLAYDIFF
            

            

              #430500invoice.payment_failed
              ⟲ REPLAY ×3EXPORT
            

          

        

        

          
STEP 03

          
Mutate & break it

          
Edit any payload before it re-fires: flip currencies, corrupt signatures, send 2 MB of JSON. Then see how your handler answers 503s, timeouts and retries before a provider ever does it for you.

          

            
status: "paid" → "failed"

            

              inject:
              503TIMEOUT 10sDROP BODYBAD HMAC
            

          

        

        

          
STEP 04

          
Share the session

          
Hand the whole capture to a teammate with a signed link — full event history, timing and your annotations. Support can see the exact payload a customer received. Export to HAR whenever a paper trail helps.

          

            
@mira pinned #431 · "currency arrives as string, not cents"

            

              @mira · editing
              @devon · viewing
              EXPORT .HAR
            

          

        

        

          
STEP 05

          
Ship the handler

          
When the timeline looks boring, generate the handler code for the events you actually captured — signature verification, idempotency checks and error paths included. Paste it in, promote the endpoint, done.

          

            $ patchbay snippet 431 --lang node

            ✔ handler.ts written · verify() · dedupe() · respond()
          

        

      

    

  







  

    

      
02 — CAPABILITIES

      
Everything a webhook wishesit could tell you.

    

    

      

        

        CORE
        
Event inspector

        
Pretty-printed bodies, raw bytes, full headers and the exact millisecond each event landed. Filter by source, status or header, and pin anything worth arguing about.

        
{ "id": "evt_4f8a2c", "type": "payment_intent.succeeded",
  "data": { "amount": 4900, "currency": "usd" },
  "received_at": "2025-06-14T09:41:22.318Z" }

      

      

        

        CORE
        
Replay engine

        
Re-deliver any captured event — one at a time or as a jittered batch — with sub-50 ms turnaround. Reproduce the exact sequence that broke staging on a Tuesday.

      

      

        

        FAILURE LAB
        
Payload mutator

        
Diff-edit any JSON before it re-fires: change amounts, drop fields, corrupt a signature, triple the size. The sender never knows; your handler learns everything.

      

      

        

        FAILURE LAB
        
Failure injection

        
Make your endpoint answer 503, stall for 10 seconds, drop the body mid-stream or respond with malformed JSON. Providers retry weirdly — rehearse for it on your terms, not theirs.

      

      

        

        TEAM
        
Shared sessions

        
One signed link hands engineering and support the same event history, timing chart and comments. No more "can you paste the payload again?" in Slack.
