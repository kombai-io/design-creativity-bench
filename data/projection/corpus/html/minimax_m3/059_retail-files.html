Decision Brief · Subject: Still-asset file format

Standardise the master delivery format for product photography, Holiday 2025 catalogue

The Standards Council has been asked to commit to a single still-asset interchange format for the Holiday 2025 catalogue — 41,002 active SKUs and 142 contracted vendor studios. The current mixed environment costs the business an estimated £312,400 per cycle in transcoding, audit rework and shelf-on-line reshoots, and obscures the provenance chain for 17% of hero assets by the time they reach the website. This brief compares six candidate formats against cost, provenance integrity, vendor readiness and 10-year archival outlook, and surfaces a recommendation that the Council can sign by Friday.

3.1 · TIFF — the uncompressed master

The TIFF baseline remains the de facto archival master at Halberd Mercantile.[1] Every product photography brief since the 1987 catalogue has specified an uncompressed, 16-bit ProPhoto RGB TIFF with embedded XMP/IPTC envelopes. The format is verifiable, lossless, supported by every vendor in our roster, and tolerant of repeated re-encoding across the studio pipeline. It is also, as the failure above makes plain, enormous. The median Holiday 2025 master weighs 247.3 MB, against an industry average of 188 MB for comparable still assets,[1] and at 41,002 SKUs end-to-end this adds 21.4 TB of intermediate storage per cycle and an estimated £87,200 of transcoding labour to bring assets down to a web-viewable size.[2]

What TIFF buys us, beyond compatibility, is provability. The XMP/IPTC envelope signed at capture is preserved verbatim through every downstream transformation. The Standards Council considered in March 2022 whether to migrate the master itself to a smaller wrapper, and rejected the proposal on the explicit ground that any migration of the master would, in the words of the Internal Audit note from 22 March 2022, "interrupt the integrity of the audit trail for assets still under retention."[6] The recommendation in §5 is therefore not to replace the master but to choose a smaller, faster delivery format that the master will be transcoded into at publish time.

"We do not propose to disturb the master. We propose to stop asking the master to do work it was never designed for — namely, to render in a browser." — M. K. Park, Director of Product Data, opening remarks, working group 09 Oct 2025

3.2 · PNG — the lossless workhorse

PNG is the obvious contender for any "smaller lossless" replacement, and it would be the easy answer if our catalogue were a tech company's component library. It is not. PNG delivers 132.6 MB at our hero resolution — a 46% reduction on TIFF — but still seven times the size of the most aggressive candidate, and the format has no signed-metadata envelope, which means the XMP/IPTC block has to ride as a sidecar XMP file with the usual consequences for audit-chain coherence.[5] PNG remains appropriate for line-art, charts and clip-art components, and we propose to retain it for those use cases. It is not appropriate for hero photography at this catalogue's resolution and lifecycle.

3.3 · JPEG (Q92) — the legacy lossy

JPEG is the format that most of the website is currently served in. It is also the format that most of the audit complaints are filed against. The 0.8% per-cycle re-encode loss compounds: an asset that has been through four down-cycles for social, mobile, print and shelf-on-line has lost an estimated 3.1% of its high-frequency detail by the time a customer sees it.[3] This is acceptable for hero imagery that will be viewed at 800 px on a phone, and unacceptable for hero imagery that will be viewed at 1,800 px on a connected display. JPEG is not the answer for the Holiday 2025 cycle; it will continue to be served, via content negotiation, as a final-mile fallback for browsers that cannot accept either of the recommended formats, and nothing in this brief should be read as proposing to remove JPEG from the delivery stack entirely.

3.4 · WebP — the modern lossless

WebP lossless is the format that the largest CDN operators have already converged on for general web delivery, and at 52.4 MB per hero it would cut our median delivery size by 79% against TIFF with no perceptual loss.[1] Browser support is at 97.1% globally and at 99.4% across our measured customer base.[4] Vendor tooling is at 78% readiness, with 31 studios — predominantly second-tier domestic partners — reporting that they do not yet export to WebP natively and would need to ship a second sidecar file.[7] The principal reservation, from Procurement, is that WebP's patent and royalty situation remains, in their words, "the kind of background risk that Internal Audit likes to write memos about,"[8] and we share that reservation. WebP remains an acceptable fallback if the recommended path cannot be cleared, and is the format named as the canonical fallback in §5.

3.5 · AVIF — the modern lossy

AVIF at Q70, 10-bit, is the format that scores best on every measurable axis that matters to the Council. It delivers a 9.1 MB hero — a 96% reduction against TIFF — with measured SSIM2 of 0.974 against the uncompressed master and a measured CIEDE2000 ΔE of 0.62 at 1,800 px, both well within the perceptual threshold that the Brand team set in 2024.[1][3] Re-encode loss is 0.4% per cycle, lower than JPEG, and the format preserves a 10-bit pipeline end-to-end which means we can deliver wide-gamut assets without an additional working-space conversion.[3]

The two reservations are candidly stated. First, vendor tooling readiness sits at 41%. Of the 142 contracted studios, only 58 export AVIF natively; the remaining 84 would, in the twelve weeks before the Holiday 2025 publish, need to install libaom-av1 or its commercial equivalent and validate output against our reference renderer.[7] Second, browser support at 94.6% globally is lower than the rest of the field, and at 88.4% across our measured customer base the gap is meaningful.[4] The recommendation in §5 is to use AVIF as the canonical delivery format, with a WebP fallback served from the same URL via content negotiation, so that the 5.4% of customers on browsers without AVIF support continue to receive a perceptually equivalent asset.

"AVIF + WebP via content negotiation is the boring answer. It is also the answer that gets us off the format treadmill for at least five years." — Dr. R. V. Okonkwo, CTO, working group minutes 11 Oct 2025

3.6 · JPEG XL — the next-gen lossless

JPEG XL is the format we would have chosen if we were choosing today for a 2030 horizon rather than a Holiday 2025 horizon. It is the only candidate that delivers a true lossless 16-bit working pipeline at 41.7 MB per hero, and its support for layered re-encodes — i.e. lossless transcoding from a JPEG source — is the closest thing the format space has to a genuine archival migration path.[1] It is also the candidate with the weakest vendor readiness, at 12%, and the weakest browser support, at 71.8% globally and 64.1% in our customer cohort, with the four largest browsers split 2–2 on intent to ship.[4][7] The Council's view, which we share, is that JPEG XL is the right format for our 2028 catalogue but not for this one, and we propose to schedule a follow-up review for Q3 2027 with a structured re-evaluation against AVIF at that point.

5 · Recommendation and commitment

The Standards Council recommends that Halberd Mercantile adopt AVIF (Q70, 10-bit) as the canonical delivery format for the Holiday 2025 catalogue, with WebP (lossless) served as the fallback via content negotiation. The TIFF master is retained unchanged. The recommendation is to be ratified on Friday 18 Oct 2025 at the Council meeting and committed to by the four accountable signatories below.

Primary commitment

AVIF · Q70 · 10-bit

Median delivery: 9.1 MB per hero · Cycle cost: £41,400 (–86.8% vs current) · Vendor tooling uplift required at 84 of 142 studios by 31 Oct 2025.

Fallback (content-negotiated)

WebP · lossless

Served automatically for browsers without AVIF support. Median delivery: 52.4 MB · Vendor tooling readiness: 78% · Royalty exposure noted in §3.4.

Recommended path

Adopt AVIF at Q70, 10-bit, as the canonical delivery format for the Holiday 2025 catalogue. Vendor tooling uplift to be completed by 31 Oct 2025, supported by a £48,000 onboarding grant administered by Procurement and a reference renderer hosted by the Standards Lab. WebP (lossless) to be served as the fallback via content negotiation at the CDN edge. The TIFF master remains unchanged and continues to be retained for seven years from capture per the existing policy.

Internal Audit to perform a conformance review at the end of the Holiday 2025 cycle (no later than 28 Feb 2026) to verify (a) that every published hero asset has an intact provenance chain back to the signed TIFF master, (b) that no customer cohort is being served an unacceptably degraded asset due to fallback-path misconfiguration, and (c) that vendor tooling uplift has reached at least 110 of 142 contracted studios. A short-form audit memo will be filed at AUD-2026-Q1 and shared with the Council at the first meeting thereafter.

Counter-position · led by S. Halversen (Vendor Liaison)

Three members of the working group — S. Halversen (Vendor Liaison), J. C. Whitcombe (Procurement) and one external advisor — argue that the 41% vendor tooling readiness for AVIF is a deployment risk that the £48,000 onboarding grant cannot fully mitigate, and that WebP (lossless) should be adopted as the canonical format for the Holiday 2025 cycle with AVIF scheduled for the Spring 2026 cycle once vendor readiness has climbed above 75%.

The counter-position is recorded here in full for the audit record and is reflected in the rollback plan at right. The Standards Council's view, on the balance of evidence, is that the counter-position understates the cost of waiting — principally the £41,400 vs £102,600 cycle-cost differential and the additional eight weeks of fallback-format transcoding that would be required — but we acknowledge the risk and have built the rollback plan accordingly. The minority view will be reproduced verbatim in the audit bundle appendix E.

Rollback plan

If fewer than 110 of 142 contracted studios have validated AVIF export by 31 Oct 2025, the Standards Council will revert the canonical delivery format to WebP (lossless) for the Holiday 2025 cycle and reschedule the AVIF migration to Spring 2026. The TIFF master and the associated provenance chain are not affected by either decision path and remain under the existing seven-year retention policy.

The rollback decision is to be made by the Director of Product Data in consultation with the CTO and recorded in the Standards Council minutes within 24 hours of the trigger date. A standing weekly checkpoint between Procurement and Vendor Liaison will run from 14 Oct 2025 to 31 Oct 2025 to track the threshold and pre-empt surprise; the checkpoint log is retained in the audit bundle appendix F.
