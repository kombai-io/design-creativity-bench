§1 · Case brief · revised 14 March 2025 · author A. Diallo

What this case is about, and why the choice matters now

The Camphill Sahel Initiative (CSI) is in the final eighteen months of a four-year integrated programme spanning water, sanitation, maternal nutrition and women's micro-enterprise across twelve communes in the Mopti region of Mali. With the donor endline window opening on 03 March 2026, the regional M&E team must commit by 30 April 2025 to the methodology that will be used to capture the programme's contribution to household food resilience — and particularly the as-yet-unmeasured role of the 2,140-woman micro-enterprise cohort enrolled between 2022 and 2024.

The choice is consequential because the donor's endline template (DCED 2024, Annex C §4.2) is unforgiving about attribution claims in conflict-affected, low-literacy settings where counterfactual baselines are weak. A methodology that over-claims causal language will trigger a downgrade to "promising practice" rather than "demonstrated contribution", reducing the carry-over weight in the next funding cycle. A methodology that under-claims will fail to surface the cohort's own narrative of recovery, which is precisely what the donor's country director has asked to see. Either failure mode costs us something the next case cannot recover, and the window for re-doing the choice closes with the 30 April lock.

This workbench exists to make that trade-off visible, line by line, before the decision is committed. The prose below is dense because the choice is dense; the affordances for editing are deliberately quiet so that they do not compete with the reading.

§2 · Decision question · owner H. Ouédraogo · re-confirmed 11 March

What, precisely, are we asking the methodology to tell us?

In one sentence: What insight methodology will best capture the contribution of the women's micro-enterprise cohort to household food resilience, in time for the donor endline in March 2026, and in language that survives both technical and narrative scrutiny?

The sentence is unpacked into four sub-questions that any chosen methodology must answer to, even if imperfectly. Each carries a weight set during stage 02 by the country director's office, and re-confirmed last week by Hamidou Ouédraogo, the regional donor liaison. The weights are: contribution 35%, mechanism 25%, distribution 20%, durability 20%.

First, contribution: how much of the observed change in the lean-season dietary diversity score (DDS-lean) among households in the cohort can plausibly be attributed to the enterprise activities, as distinct from concurrent rainfall recovery, the IMF-monitored millet subsidy, and the spontaneous labour migration that returned after the 2023 ceasefire? Second, mechanism: through which of the four hypothesised pathways — income, social standing, time-use, or intra-household bargaining — does any contribution actually travel? Third, distribution: does the contribution concentrate in the better-off tercile of the cohort or reach the most vulnerable, and what does that say about the targeting model we built into the recruitment in 2022? Fourth, durability: will the contribution persist through the 2026 lean season if the supplementary feeding component is withdrawn, as scheduled, in October 2025?

If a methodology cannot speak to at least three of the four sub-questions without forcing us to add a parallel study, it should be downgraded, not patched. Patching in a parallel study costs us the credibility we are trying to buy.

§3 · Population & stakeholders · field-verified 02 March 2025

Whose insight, and to whose eyes, will the work be read?

The cohort is 2,140 women in twelve communes; 64% are illiterate in any language, 22% read Songhai or Bambara at a basic level, and 14% are functionally literate in French. Mean age is 34 (range 17–61). 41% belong to a female-headed household. The cohort was recruited from a pre-existing list of 5,800 households that received the WASH platform in 2022; the enterprise arm is the only differentiated layer and was added in three staggered cohorts of roughly 700 each.

The stakeholders who will read whatever we produce are more demanding than the cohort is varied. The donor country director wants a single page of narrative that a non-technical minister could absorb in three minutes. The donor's independent evaluator wants a methodological annex with enough rigour to be cited in their sectoral review. The Mopti regional health directorate wants disaggregated indicators by commune and by pregnant-or-lactating status, in French, on the fifth of every month. The cohort itself, through its twelve elected lead-farmers, has asked that the methodology include a forum in which the women themselves can disagree with our framing. The ethics advisory committee will need a full re-submission if we move from a one-shot retrospective to a longitudinal design.

§4 · Candidate methodologies · five short-listed on stage 04

Five methodologies survived stage 04's gating criteria — minimum two published applications in Sahel or comparable contexts; budget envelope under €140,000; deliverable language English, French or Bambara; methodology licence compatible with our open-data commitment. §5 · Read-outs on the two front-runners

What Outcome Harvesting and the Sentinel Panel each give us — and what each hides

Outcome Harvesting — the case for it

Outcome Harvesting is the methodology the regional team has the most lived experience with: it was used in 2021 for the WASH-platform endline and produced the only donor-accepted contribution narrative in CSI's history. Its core mechanic — backward-looking identification of outcomes among the intervention's boundary partners, then forward-tracing of which intervention most plausibly contributed — is well matched to the question we are asking, because the four hypothesised pathways (income, standing, time-use, bargaining) are exactly the kind of "soft" contributions that the donor's narrative template was written to capture. OH scores high on mechanism and on distribution, because it forces the team to interrogate who in the cohort changed, not merely whether anyone did. The 2021 application surfaced two outcomes that the donor's evaluator described, in the final report, as "the most credible contribution evidence produced by a Sahel programme in this funding cycle".

Its costs are well rehearsed in the team. Six weeks of enumerator training (40 enumerators, three cohorts of two weeks each), twelve weeks of harvesting rounds in the communes, six weeks of substantiation and triangulation against administrative data, four weeks of write-up. The deliverable is a structured narrative portfolio of 60–80 outcomes, each accompanied by a contribution scorecard and a substantiation note. The methodology's defensible weakness is its handling of the durability sub-question. OH is, by design, retrospective: it harvests what has already happened, and is conservative about projecting forward. Our durability sub-question is essentially prospective (will the contribution persist after October 2025?), and OH does not pretend to answer it. We would need a small forward-looking supplement — a quarterly phone panel of 240 cohort members across the remaining eighteen months — to honour the sub-question. That supplement adds €11,200 and three weeks, both of which fit comfortably inside the envelope and the calendar.

Sentinel Site Longitudinal Panel — the case for it

The Sentinel Site Longitudinal Panel is the methodology the independent evaluator, Mariam Sangaré, has privately indicated a preference for. It is the most quantitatively credible of the five: it gives us a genuine panel, with the same 480 households interviewed at five points across 2024–2026, allowing within-household change to be tracked at the indicator level (DDS-lean, HDDS, women's decision-making score, lean-season coping strategy index). On durability — the sub-question OH cannot answer cleanly — the Panel is the only methodology that does so by construction. It also gives Dr. Koné's directorate the monthly disaggregated indicators they have asked for, with no extra work, which removes a parallel-track obligation from the procurement office in Sévaré.

Its costs are heavier and its risks sharper. The Panel requires a baseline that is already two-thirds lost: the original 2022 baseline was designed for a different evaluation question and lacks the enterprise-cohort identifiers we now need. A retrofit baseline would cost €38,000 and consume four of the 34 weeks. It is also the only methodology on the short-list that exposes us to attrition risk in conflict-affected zones: we can credibly model 12% annual attrition in two of the twelve communes (Douentza, Koro), which would push the panel below the 400-household threshold the donor requires for within-group inference. A mitigation reserve of €22,000 to oversample and trace migrant households is realistic, not generous, and would still leave us exposed to a single bad security week in Bandiagara.

§6 · Cost, time, and feasibility envelopes

What the budget and the calendar will actually permit

The methodology envelope, set in the stage 04 gate, is €140,000 inclusive of evaluation staff time, travel, enumerator contracts, transcription, translation, and a 12% contingency. The delivery window is 18 months to a March 2026 endline submission. Both front-runners fit the envelope, but neither comfortably. The figures below are from the procurement office in Sévaré as of 09 March 2025, and have not been re-negotiated since.

Outcome Harvesting — total cost

€84,100 — core €72,900 plus forward-looking supplement €11,200. 25 weeks to delivery. Contingency reserve €9,900 available for re-runs of substantiation panels in three communes where 2021 reliability was marginal (Bandiagara, Bankass, Koro).

Sentinel Panel — total cost

€160,500 — core €128,500 plus retrofit baseline €38,000, less €6,000 reuse of WASH baseline instruments. 34 weeks to delivery. €20,500 over envelope. Closure options are a reduced panel (320 households, drops inference confidence), a smaller oversample reserve, or a request to the country director for an exceptional €22,000 uplift.

Calendar pressure

OH leaves 9 weeks of margin to the donor submission window; the Panel leaves −2 weeks. The Panel therefore requires either a delay request to the donor (politically costly) or a parallel fast-stream track for the most-cited indicators (methodologically fragile, and the kind of compromise the evaluator notices).

Team capacity

OH uses the existing 14-person M&E team at full stretch for 25 weeks — manageable, and consistent with how the team worked in 2021. The Panel requires an additional 3 contract analysts for 30 weeks and a part-time biometric-data manager not currently in the CSI organogram. Recruitment lead time from the Sévaré office: 11 weeks. That lead time consumes the entire margin left in the calendar.

Procurement dependencies

OH depends on one piece of unfamiliar software (the Outcome Harvesting sub-system in KoBoToolbox) and on a translation workflow we already use. The Panel depends on biometric tablet procurement (lead time 9 weeks, single supplier), a comms plan for households unreachable by road, and a data-protection impact assessment that has not yet been started.

Cohort burden

OH asks each household for between 45 minutes and 2 hours once, with optional follow-up. The Panel asks each of 480 households for 90 minutes five times. The cohort's lead-farmers have indicated, in informal conversation, that the lower-burden option is the one they will defend in the November forum.

§7 · Risk, ethics and data sensitivity

What could go wrong, and what we are obligated to protect

Two risk classes dominate this selection, and the choice between methodologies shifts which risk class bites hardest. Attribution risk is the risk that the methodology produces a contribution claim that the donor's independent evaluator downgrades. OH's narrative-anchored contribution is harder to falsify in print but easier to discredit in seminar — the kind of seminar in which the evaluator convenes a peer reviewer and asks, plainly, "how do you know this wasn't the millet subsidy?" The Panel's indicator-anchored contribution is harder to discredit in seminar but easier to fault on attrition and baseline quality — the kind of fault line that surfaces twelve months after fieldwork, in a footnote, when it is too late to fix. Either failure has a known shape; the question is which shape we are better placed to absorb.

Participant risk is the risk that the methodology itself exposes cohort members to harm. The Panel's repeated visits to the same 480 households, in two communes with active armed-group presence, are a more credible threat to participant safety than OH's distributed harvesting rounds, which never require the same household to be visited more than twice. The Panel also raises a duty-of-care question we have not yet answered: what does the field team owe a household that, having been visited five times across two years, has come to be associated in the village with "the people from the NGO who come with tablets"? OH, by contrast, distributes contact across the cohort and limits identification risk per household.

The cohort's own lead-farmers, in their November 2024 forum, asked the regional team to "let the women speak first, and count them second". That instruction weighs against the Panel and in favour of OH, though not decisively. The ethics advisory committee will require a full re-submission if the Panel is chosen, on the basis that the longitudinal design raises the bar on informed consent, withdrawal mechanics, and the duty of care owed during any incident in the field. The re-submission will not be fast, and the calendar cannot absorb a long ethics pause.

Cohort burden and participant safety both tilt the same direction. Ethics review timing tilts the same direction harder. The methodological case for the Panel is real; the institutional case against it is heavier.

What I am recommending, in language the workbench can defend

The recommendation is provisional. It becomes a decision at stage 06 once signed by the country director, the independent evaluator, and the cohort representative. The label below is being edited live; the previous value is preserved in the audit log (revision 3.1, 13 March).

Recommended approach label · inline edit active · author A. Diallo Unsaved

Outcome Harvesting as the primary methodology, supplemented by a quarterly phone panel of 240 cohort members to honour the durability sub-question. The recommendation prioritises mechanism and distribution (where OH scores 3 / 4 on each) over the marginal rigour gain of a panel that would push us €20,500 over envelope, recruit outside our existing organogram, and re-open the ethics committee review. The donor-facing narrative is expected to be more honest, more legible to the cohort, and easier for the cohort to contest if we get it wrong.

If the country director prefers the Sentinel Panel on durability grounds, the request for an exceptional €22,000 uplift should accompany the methodology selection, not follow it. Asking for the uplift after the fact will read as an afterthought and is the single most common reason donor-side methodology choices get downgraded on arrival.
