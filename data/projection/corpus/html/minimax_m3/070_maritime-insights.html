Conclusion & recommendation

Re-allocate four additional dual-fuel units from the FE3 rotation onto the AEX-1 / AEX-7 loops for Q4, lifting the LNG share of Asia–N. Europe sailings from 58 % to 71 %. Lock in Q1 2025 bunker requirements at the current Brent-curve ahead of the November IMO carbon-intensity review. We expect an incremental USD 19.4 M in operating profit across the four-vessel transfer.

What changed since Q2

Drivers behind the headline move.

  • ↑
    Spot rate index recovered on Asia–N. Europe

    SCFI Asia–Europe component closed Q3 at 1,884 USD/TEU, up 14.2 % QoQ. Front-haul demand held through July–August on inventory restocking into Rotterdam and Hamburg.

  • ↓
    VLSFO eased while LNG optionality widened

    VLSFO Rotterdam fell 8.3 % QoQ to USD 538/MT. LNG dual-fuel burn saved an average 41 t of CO₂ per transit and reduced fuel cost by USD 11,400 per voyage for the 14 LNG-capable units.

  • !
    Suez transit reliability is no longer a tail-risk

    Sailing via Suez held 94.6 % reliability this quarter, but the recommended Cape route redundancy is preserved for two vessels owing to residual Houthit-zone exposure.

  • !
    Three VLSFO-only vessels still in AEX-1 rotation

    MV Hibernian Tide, MV Pacific Crest and MV Northern Voyager require bunker-hedge refresh before 2024-11-15 or face Q4 ETS exposure of an additional EUR 0.7 M combined.

  • ★
    Cape Sirocco & Hibernian Wave set personal records

    Both vessels completed Shanghai–Rotterdam in under 31 days for the first time. Cape Sirocco (27d 22h) became the fastest transit in the cohort this quarter.
