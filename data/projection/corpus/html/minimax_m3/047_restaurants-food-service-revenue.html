Section 03 · From the editor — Issue 14

Why we started indexing restaurant revenue in the first place.

Before Settle existed, I ran a 48-seat restaurant in Sligo called Larkin's. Every quarter I'd sit down with my accountant and try to figure out whether the decisions I was making on the line were actually showing up in the books.

I would call other operators I knew, but the data they could share was always a few months stale and always hedged — "we're up" or "we're flat" — never specific enough to act on. The first version of this library was a shared spreadsheet between fourteen operators in the northwest of Ireland. We agreed to log our weekly covers, average ticket, and food cost percentage on the same Monday each week. After six months we had a dataset that was small, but real, and for the first time I could see how my Tuesday lunch covers compared to a comparable room in Donegal.

That spreadsheet became the database. The database became this library. What you are reading right now is the public preview — the gate at the top of the page is real, and it does what it says. Apply for an account and the seventeen locked rows in the table above will resolve into actual figures inside an hour.

Three primary sources, one backup.

Every revenue figure in the table has been confirmed against at least one of three primary sources. We do not accept self-reported numbers without a corresponding document on file, even when the operator insists their books are correct. The three primary sources, in order of how often we use them:

Filed sales-tax returns. About 38 US states publish monthly or quarterly sales-tax filings in a machine-readable format. The operator's name on the filing must match the operator's name on the Settle profile; we do not aggregate, we do not impute, and we do not round to the nearest thousand.

Signed CPA attestations. A letter on the CPA's letterhead, signed and dated within the last 90 days, stating the operator's total revenue for the relevant quarter. Roughly 41% of the cohort uses this method. The CPA does not need to be a Settle member.

Bank-statement exports through Plaid. Read-only access, scoped to a single business account and a single quarter. We never see the operator's full banking history; the integration is purpose-built for quarterly verification and is the method most preferred by multi-unit operators because it requires no paperwork.

What we don't do.

We don't collect tips, we don't ask for point-of-sale data, we don't accept screenshots from a back-of-house iPad, and we don't infer revenue from a third-party delivery platform's public commission statements. We also don't take advertising, we don't have a sales team, and we don't take referral fees from any of the platforms mentioned in the operators' notes. If you find an error in the table, write to corrections@settle.co with the operator ID and we will publish a public revision note inside seven days.

Halcyon & Hearth — Brooklyn, NY · Modern British.

Four locations across Brooklyn and Queens, opened between 2019 and 2023. Q3 2024 revenue $1,847,302 across all sites, +14.2% year over year, driven almost entirely by a Wednesday-only tasting-menu night the chef rolled out in March. Average ticket $42.18. Food cost held at 28.4% — Marcus attributes this to a single supplier change on the lamb program. Read the full operator note in the library.

The Marigold Kitchen — Hudson, NY · Farm-to-table.

One 32-seat room plus a six-seat chef's counter, opened January 2023. Q3 2024 revenue $1,512,008, +22.1% year over year — the highest YoY growth in the current cohort for single-location operators. The owner, Trish Blackwell, attributes it to a deliberate price increase in July ($52 → $58 average ticket) combined with a 6% drop in covers: net positive, net happier customers, net the same labour cost.

Birchwood Tavern — Bozeman, MT · American.

Five locations across Montana and Idaho, the oldest in the cohort (joined June 2020). Q3 2024 revenue $1,298,604, +4.2% year over year. Birchwood is the cohort's most-cited benchmark for operators trying to scale beyond three sites without losing margin. The 4.2% growth rate, modest as it sounds, is the median for multi-site operators in the library; the dispersion is what is interesting, and the dispersion is what the unlocked columns let you see.

The Copper Pot — Galway, IE · Irish.

One 36-seat room with an eight-seat bar, opened March 2023. Q3 2024 revenue sits inside the locked rows above; the operator's note (unlocked for verified members) describes the +12.0% YoY figure as "almost entirely a function of getting off Uber Eats in November 2023 and putting that 28% back into our own margins." The Copper Pot is the smallest operator in the top quartile of the cohort.

Why do you have a gate at all?

Because the operators who shared their books asked us to. The free preview is intentionally limited to eight profiles per month, randomised at the start of each calendar month. If you read carefully you'll notice the locked rows in the table above show you the operator name, cuisine, location count and join date — just not the dollar figures. That's deliberate, not an oversight.

Can my restaurant be removed from the library?

Yes. If your restaurant is in the dataset and you'd rather your profile not appear at all, write to redact@settle.co with your operator ID and we will suppress the profile within 48 hours. If you'd like to remove only the preview visibility but keep your full profile active for verified members, that toggle is in your account settings under Privacy → Public preview.

Can I bring my own accountant?

Yes. Verified members can add a read-only seat for an outside accountant at no charge. We have about 240 accountants and CPAs in the directory; if yours isn't one of them, they can apply through the same form at the top of the page.

Do you offer a free tier?

The eight-profile monthly preview is the free tier. There is no card-on-file option, no trial, no "free for the first 14 days," and no auto-renew. Either you read the eight profiles a month we choose for you, or you pay the $480 annual fee and read everything.

What if my accountant and I disagree on a number?

Write to us. About 4% of the cohort has filed a formal correction request at some point; the average correction shifts the relevant quarter by less than 1.4%, and we publish the original figure, the corrected figure, and the date of correction in the public revision log.

Methodology · v3.2

How a number gets into the library

Every revenue figure in the Settle library has been confirmed against at least one of the following primary sources. We do not accept self-reported numbers without a corresponding document on file.

  1. Filed sales-tax return US states that publish monthly sales-tax filings — about 38 as of October 2024. The operator's name on the filing must match the operator's name on the Settle profile, and the filing must cover at least 11 of the 13 weeks in the relevant quarter.
  2. Signed CPA attestation A letter on the CPA's letterhead, signed and dated within the last 90 days, stating the operator's total revenue for the relevant quarter. Roughly 41% of the cohort uses this method. The CPA does not need to be a Settle member.
  3. Bank-statement export through Plaid Read-only access, limited to a single business account, scoped to the quarter in question. We never see the operator's full banking history. This is the method most preferred by multi-unit operators because it requires no paperwork.

If none of those three are available, we do not include the operator in the library. Operators whose primary source has lapsed are moved to the bottom of the sort order and flagged with an amber dot — they are not removed, so historical comparisons remain stable.
