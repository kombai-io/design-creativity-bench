Section 1 of 7

Summary of the committee’s findings

Following a sixteen-week investigation, including five committee sessions and the review of 142 numbered exhibits, the Faculty–Student Grievance Committee finds the grievance substantiated in part and orders the remedies described in the full decision.

The grievance, filed by Ms. Marisa Trent on 04 March 2024, raised three concerns. First, that Ms. Trent was removed from authorship of a manuscript submitted to the Journal of Structural Biology in February 2024 despite having contributed substantially to the underlying experiments and analysis. Second, that Dr. Henrik Voss had failed to provide timely, substantive mentorship during the 2023–24 academic year in ways that impaired her progress toward the dissertation. Third, that a reassignment of laboratory bench space on 12 January 2024 was retaliatory in nature.

The committee finds the first concern substantiated, the second concern substantiated in part, and the third concern not substantiated. Dr. Voss is directed to take the corrective actions set out in §4 of the full decision; the Department of Molecular and Cellular Biology is directed to undertake the structural changes set out in §5. The remedies take effect on 02 December 2024 unless modified on appeal.

What happens next The remedies take effect on 02 December 2024. You have thirty days from receipt of this notice to file a written appeal with the University Appeals Board. Please see the Your rights & next steps section for the procedure, the form, and the applicable deadlines.

The full written decision

The text below reproduces the operative portions of the committee’s decision in full, preserving the section numbering used in the sealed original. Footnotes and references to exhibits are preserved for cross-reference with the case archive.

§1. Jurisdiction and standard of review

The committee acts pursuant to Sections IV through VII of the Faculty–Student Grievance Procedures (revised July 2022). The applicable standard is whether, on the preponderance of the evidence, the respondent engaged in conduct that violated the University Statement on Authorship in Scholarly Publication, the Graduate Mentorship Compact of 2018, or the University Non-Retaliation Policy. The committee’s review is de novo as to the factual record and deferential as to academic judgement, in accordance with §IV.B.3 of the Procedures.

§2. Findings of fact

Ms. Trent joined the Trent Laboratory in September 2021 under a five-year graduate research fellowship. Between September 2022 and January 2024 she contributed to the design, execution, and analysis of the experiments reported in manuscript MCB-2023-118 (the “Manuscript”), including twelve of the forty-three reported experiments, the statistical analysis presented in Figure 4, and the drafting of the methods section in substantially the form in which it was submitted. Her contributions are documented in laboratory notebook TR-04 (Exhibits 28–31) and were confirmed by the testimony of Dr. Réal Ouellette and Dr. Sun-Hee Park.

On 19 February 2024, Dr. Voss submitted the Manuscript to the Journal of Structural Biology with Ms. Trent listed third among seven authors. Email correspondence of 21 February 2024 (Exhibit 47) shows that Dr. Voss removed Ms. Trent from an earlier draft in which she had appeared as second author. The committee finds that no contemporaneous reason was given for the change and that the only documented justification, a sentence in an email of 03 March 2024 referring to “a sense of ownership on R.H.’s part,” was supplied after the grievance had been filed and is not corroborated by the underlying record.

§3. Conclusions of law

The committee concludes that Ms. Trent’s removal from the Manuscript was contrary to the Statement on Authorship, which requires that all individuals who have made substantial intellectual contributions to a work be offered co-authorship unless the individual declines in writing. No written declination is in the record, and no contemporaneous evidence supports a finding that Ms. Trent was offered and declined authorship. The committee further concludes that Dr. Voss’s mentorship during the period from September 2023 to February 2024 fell below the standard set by the Graduate Mentorship Compact, particularly with respect to the holding of scheduled one-on-one meetings and the timely return of substantive feedback on dissertation chapters. The committee does not find, on the evidence presented, that the reassignment of bench space on 12 January 2024 was retaliatory; the departmental record (Exhibit 76) reflects a documented renovation of MCB-318 that affected all occupants of the bench tier.

§4. Remedies ordered against the respondent

Dr. Voss is directed, within thirty days of the date of this resolution, to (a) submit a corrected authorship list for the Manuscript restoring Ms. Trent to second author, and to notify the Journal of Structural Biology of the correction in accordance with the journal’s stated policy on authorship disputes; (b) issue a written acknowledgement to Ms. Trent, copied to the department chair, that her contributions to the Manuscript qualify her for co-authorship under the University Statement on Authorship; and (c) meet with the Vice Provost for Graduate Education to develop a written mentorship plan for the 2024–25 academic year, with quarterly check-ins documented in writing.

§5. Structural remedies directed to the department

The Department of Molecular and Cellular Biology is directed to (a) re-convene the Manuscript authorship review and offer Ms. Trent co-authorship on the published version of MCB-2023-118; (b) appoint an interim mentorship committee of two tenured faculty members from outside the Department of Molecular and Cellular Biology to provide external mentorship support for the remainder of the 2024–25 academic year; and (c) report on implementation of these structural remedies to the Provost by 31 January 2025.

Section 7 of 7

Frequently asked questions

The questions below are the ones most often raised in the days following a resolution. If your question is not addressed here, the Grievance Office is the appropriate point of contact.

Does my acknowledgment of the resolution affect my right to appeal?

No. Acknowledging receipt confirms only that you have received and reviewed the resolution. It does not waive your right to appeal, to seek review of a remedy, or to request accommodations for any deadline. The acknowledgment is recorded separately from any appeal filings and has no bearing on the merits of any later challenge.

Who can see the materials I submitted during the grievance?

The committee members, the parties, and the Provost’s designee have access to the case file during the proceedings. Records are retained for seven years in the secure case archive and are not disclosed except as required by the Grievance Procedures, by court order, or with your written consent. Routine internal reporting does not name individual grievants.

What remedies has the committee ordered the department to implement?

Section 5 of the decision directs the department to (i) re-convene the Manuscript authorship review and offer Ms. Trent co-authorship on the published version of MCB-2023-118; (ii) appoint an interim mentorship committee of two tenured faculty from outside the Department of Molecular and Cellular Biology for the remainder of the 2024–25 academic year; and (iii) report on implementation to the Provost by 31 January 2025.

Can I withdraw my grievance at this stage?

No. Once the committee has issued a resolution, the matter is closed unless modified on appeal. The Grievance Procedures do not permit withdrawal of a grievance after a resolution has been issued; they do, however, permit the parties to file a joint stipulation narrowing the remedies, which the Provost may accept or decline.

What if I have new evidence that became available after the resolution?

You may file a motion to reopen the record with the Provost’s designee. Motions to reopen are granted only on a showing that the evidence was not reasonably available during the original proceedings and that its consideration would reasonably affect the outcome. The standard is set out in §VII.D of the Procedures.
