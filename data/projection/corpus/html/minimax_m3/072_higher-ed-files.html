The Differential Impact of Asynchronous Discussion Forums on Metacognitive Engagement in Hybrid Undergraduate Engineering Courses

P. Ramaswamy, E. Castellanos-Vélez, A. Sørensen & M. T. Holloway · Journal of Engineering Education, Special issue on hybrid pedagogy · 14,287 / 18,000 wds  ·  42 pp  ·  6 figures  ·  63 references

§3.2

Participants and Sampling

Auto-graded 5 of 5 reviewers

We recruited students from four sections of a sophomore-level Introduction to Thermodynamics course taught at a single research-intensive institution in the northeastern United States. Across the three-year study window (Autumn 2020 through Spring 2023), 412 students consented to participate; 387 of those completed all required instruments and are included in the analyses reported here. The remaining 25 either withdrew from the course (n = 14) or failed to complete the post-instruction metacognitive measure (n = 11). 1

Cohort sizes varied modestly year over year. Autumn 2020 enrolled 94 students (47 in the treatment condition, 47 in control); Autumn 2021 enrolled 152 students (78 treatment, 74 control); and Spring 2023 enrolled 141 students (72 treatment, 69 control). The Spring 2022 term was excluded from analysis following a mid-semester pivot to fully remote instruction in response to a regional public-health emergency, which we discuss in §5.4 as a limitation on generalisability.

“We treat the Spring 2022 cohort as a quasi-natural interruption that informs — but does not contaminate — the longitudinal pattern of effects we report.” — Authors' positionality note, §3.2.1

Treatment sections used a structured asynchronous discussion protocol developed collaboratively with course instructional staff. The protocol required students to contribute an opening post of 200–400 words by 23:59 each Wednesday, to respond substantively to at least two peers by 23:59 each Sunday, and to submit a weekly reflection entry of 100–150 words by 23:59 the following Monday. Control sections continued with the institution's existing discussion practice, which historically centred on synchronous office-hour sessions supplemented by an ungraded, low-stakes discussion board.

Power analysis conducted a priori (α = .05, power = .80) using G*Power 3.1 indicated a minimum required sample of 128 students per cohort to detect a moderate effect (Cohen's d = .35) on our primary metacognitive outcome, the Metacognitive Awareness Inventory (Schraw & Dennison, 1994). All cohorts exceeded this threshold with the exception of the Autumn 2020 cohort, for which the study period overlapped with constrained in-person instruction. We address this in §5.2 by replicating the headline analysis on the 2021 and 2023 cohorts alone and find no substantive change in the magnitude or direction of reported effects. 1

Assignment to condition was made at the section level rather than the individual level to avoid contamination across treatment and control peers enrolled in the same recitation. Institutional review board approval was granted under protocol IRB-2024-0317, with reciprocal recognition from the collaborating institutions (Universidad de los Andes; Aalborg University) under their standard cross-institutional data-sharing addenda.
