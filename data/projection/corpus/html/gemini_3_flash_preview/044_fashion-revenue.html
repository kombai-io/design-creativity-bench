Revenue Recognition & Multi-Channel Attribution

Ref: FSH-2023-REV-09 Last Updated: October 24, 2023 Effective Period: Q4 2023 - Q2 2024 Status: Active

1. Executive Summary

This document serves as the authoritative guide for administrators managing the fiscal transition between multi-channel retail sales and consolidated revenue reporting. For the fiscal year 2023, the organization has adopted an enhanced attribution model that prioritizes point-of-sale (POS) finality while accounting for the significant shift toward "Ship-from-Store" fulfillment dynamics.

As an administrator, your primary role is ensuring that the bridge between Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) and Net Revenue is reconciled within the 48-hour settlement window. This ensures that seasonal markdown strategies do not artificially inflate liquidity forecasts.

2. Primary Revenue Recognition Policy

Revenue is recognized when the control of high-fashion goods is transferred to the customer. For in-store purchases, this occurs at the moment of payment. For e-commerce transactions, control is deemed transferred upon delivery confirmation from the carrier (FOB Destination).

Note: Pre-orders for "Runway to Rack" collections are treated as deferred revenue (Liability) until the physical shipment is initiated from the regional distribution center.

3. Multi-Channel Attribution Framework

With the expansion into boutique-to-web fulfillment, attribution is now split 70/30 between the originating digital storefront and the fulfilling physical location. This ensures store managers are incentivized to maintain inventory integrity for online order picks.

Administrators must verify that the attribution flags in the settlement table below match the localized tax jurisdictions of the fulfilling store, particularly for cross-border transactions within the EU and North American markets.

5. Markdown & Discount Impact

During seasonal transitions, discount codes (PROMO20, VIP-EXCL) are applied at the basket level. For revenue recognition, these discounts are netted against the gross sales price of each SKU proportionally to its original MSRP.

Administrators should note that "Final Sale" items are exempt from the standard return provision accrual (see Section 6), and their revenue is considered finalized immediately upon transaction, regardless of the fulfillment status.

6. Returns & Reverse Logistics Accruals

Historical data suggests a 22% return rate for digital apparel sales. Consequently, a "Right of Return" asset and a corresponding refund liability are recorded at the point of sale. These accruals are adjusted monthly based on actual return performance during the 30-day customer window.

Restocking fees, where applicable, are recognized as "Service Revenue" and are not credited back to the original item's product line revenue, but rather to "Operational Recoveries."

7. Compliance & Audit Trails

All adjustments made within the settlement queue are logged with a timestamp and user ID. To remain compliant with IFRS 15, any manual override of an automated attribution must be accompanied by a justificatory comment.

External auditors perform quarterly spot checks on the "Accrual-to-Cash" bridge. Ensure all physical boutique receipts for high-value transactions (>€5,000) are digitized and attached to the digital record.

8. Escalation & Contact

For discrepancies exceeding 5% of a channel's daily volume, contact the Global Controller's office immediately. Technical issues regarding the Shopify-to-ERP bridge should be directed to the Retail Systems Support team (Priority 1 Ticket).
