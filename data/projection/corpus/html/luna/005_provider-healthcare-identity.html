The choice changes the operating model as much as the matching engine.

With VerityLink

Credentialing keeps ownership of a small, explainable exception queue. Directory and claims teams consume a stable enterprise provider ID rather than rebuilding joins in each workflow.

Without VerityLink

Each consuming team continues to reconcile its own roster. That preserves local flexibility, but keeps duplicate identifiers in circulation and makes a patient-safety investigation slower to assemble.

Guardrails

A contained bet, not a platform rewrite

We can learn enough to decide without moving the source of truth for credentialing or claims.

What we will protect

Existing provider IDs remain authoritative in each source system. The pilot writes a crosswalk and confidence score only; it does not overwrite demographics, licenses, or privileges.

What would stop us

Pause the pilot if false merges exceed 0.4%, if a high-risk specialty lacks an explainable match, or if the vendor cannot complete the BAA and penetration-test evidence by day ten.

Six-week pilot

Start narrow. Measure what matters.

Three source populations give us enough variation to test the decision without creating an enterprise migration program.

Weeks 1–2 · establish trust

Load 42,800 providers from the credentialing master, Meridian roster, and claims billing file. Security validates encryption, retention, and role-based access. Operations labels a 1,200-record benchmark set.

Weeks 3–6 · prove utility

Publish a read-only crosswalk to directory search and claims analytics. Track confidence, exception aging, and duplicate suppression weekly. The steering group reviews the go/no-go scorecard on 21 March.
