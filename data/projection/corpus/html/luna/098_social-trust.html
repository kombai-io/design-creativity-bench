Trust is a practice, not a promise

A field guide for social-sector teams that want to make accountability visible, shared and durable—especially when the work gets difficult.

Owner Amara RuizAudience Partner organisationsReading time 6 minReview due 21 Jun 2024

Purpose & scope

Core

Trust is the accumulated result of what an organisation does when no announcement is required. This framework helps teams describe the behaviours that let communities judge our work for themselves. It applies to grantmaking, direct service, research partnerships and public campaigning.

Editorial principle — Do not ask people to trust our intentions when we can show our decisions, constraints and response to feedback.

Trust signals

Evidence-led
  • ✓Publish decisions with the reason, not only the result.
  • ✓Name who was missing from the room and what changes next.
  • ✓Return findings in the format and language people requested.
  • ✓Compensate community expertise at the same rate as professional advice.
  • ✓Keep a visible record of commitments after a project closes.
  • ✓Escalate safeguarding concerns without transferring the burden.

Practice standards

For teams

Every engagement should leave a clearer trail than it found. Use the following minimum standard before a programme moves from listening to action:

  • 01Permission: explain use, retention and withdrawal before collecting a story.
  • 02Power: state what participants can influence and what is fixed.
  • 03Return: share a plain-language decision log within 30 days.
  • 04Repair: offer a route to challenge harm without relying on the project lead.

Measurement

Quarterly

Pair sentiment with proof. A higher score is not progress if people cannot identify what changed because they spoke.

Risks & safeguards

Review closely

Trust work can reproduce the power it aims to correct. Name the failure mode early, then assign a person with authority to act.

  • !Extraction: stories gathered for our narrative but never returned.
  • !Performance: listening becomes a polished substitute for redistribution.
  • !Exposure: public transparency reveals a participant’s identity.
  • !Fatigue: the same people are repeatedly asked to validate the work.
