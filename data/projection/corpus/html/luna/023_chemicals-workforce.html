01 · The readout

A steadier workforce, with pressure moving upstream

Net positive

Asterion ended the quarter with more people, fewer regretted departures and a noticeably calmer relationship between its sites and central people team. The headline growth is not a hiring spree: 164 operators and engineers joined, principally around the Rotterdam polyols complex and the Teesside additives line, while 52 agency positions were converted to permanent contracts.

The less comfortable part of the story sits before production begins. Maintenance, process control and EHS vacancies now account for 61% of the technical requisition book. Managers describe a market that has loosened for general production labour but remains stubborn for instrument technicians who understand both hazardous-area compliance and continuous-process chemistry. Asterion’s new academy is an answer, though not yet a substitute for experienced hands.

“The shift pattern is no longer the thing people leave. It is the lack of a visible next step.”

That distinction matters for a shopper comparing operators. The company has created a credible floor for continuity; it has not removed the succession risk embedded in a handful of specialist roles.

Analyst note · Helena Ward, industrials research · 12 July 2024

03 · Retention

Fewer exits, better reasons

Watch closely

Voluntary attrition fell to 7.4%, helped by a revised night-shift premium and a more transparent overtime rota. Exit interviews show “schedule predictability” overtaking pay as the leading reason to stay. The result is strongest at Grangemouth, where the local site council has been meeting monthly with shift leads.

Evidence: 318 exit interviews; 74% response rate
04 · Capability build

The academy is now a system

In motion

Asterion’s Process Chemistry Academy moved from pilot to a three-site programme. Its first 46 learners rotate through control-room simulation, permit-to-work practice and polymer quality labs. Completion is being tied to a recognised Level 3 qualification, giving early-career staff a portable credential rather than an internal badge.

46 enrolled · 19 qualified · 83% completion at checkpoint one

Movements

Across the quarter, 164 employees joined Asterion and 119 left. The largest movement was the conversion of 52 agency workers into permanent operations contracts in Rotterdam and Teesside. Internal mobility accounted for 38% of filled technical roles, up from 29% in the prior quarter.

Sources & method

This brief combines Asterion’s Q2 people ledger, vacancy snapshots taken on the final Friday of each month, anonymised exit-interview themes, site pulse notes and the group safety dashboard. Figures exclude joint ventures and are normalised for the 13-week quarter.
