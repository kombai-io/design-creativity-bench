Internal memo

How the imbalance formed

Meridian Settlement Group has been Aurora's largest originating partner for eighteen consecutive months. Their files entered the clearing pipeline under routine terms: CSV-formatted batch exports containing approximately 2,000 to 5,000 individual transaction lines per submission, each tagged with a merchant ID, amount, currency code, settlement date, and an ISO-20022-compliant remittance reference. For more than a year this format held without deviation. Then, on 12 August at roughly 02:47 UTC, their latest batch—file ASG-20240801-B17—arrived with altered headers. The remittance references no longer carried the expected prefix (MRD-). They were replaced by a sequence of alphanumeric strings that bore no relation to any naming convention in Aurora's registry. More critically, the batch-level total listed in the header field differed from the sum of the individual transaction amounts by exactly $238,910.00.

Our reconciliation engine could not match any of the subsequent Meridian submissions against known merchant accounts because the reference keys themselves had changed. This was not, initially, treated as suspicious. We assumed a systems-side issue at Meridian—a misconfigured export module, perhaps—and opened ticket ASR-7741 with their operations team. Their response, dated 20 August, was a brief acknowledgment followed by three further batches, each carrying the same structural anomalies. By 3 September, when five entire weeks of Meridian's processing volume sat unreconciled and the cumulative discrepancy had exceeded nine million dollars, we escalated internally. The finance director approved an emergency audit on 4 September. Two external auditors from Deloitte were engaged on 6 September. Neither team has completed its review.

What complicates any simple explanation is the pattern visible across the intervening weeks. Meridian's batches did not simply arrive with malformed data. On eleven separate occasions between 12 August and 1 October, the internal timestamps embedded within each transaction line preceded the batch creation date—an impossibility if the data was generated sequentially. In four of those cases, the offset was measurable in days rather than seconds. On a single occasion, ASG-20240822-D06, three of the 5,103 individual transactions carried negative amounts totalling $18,440.00, which effectively reduced the declared batch total below what would be expected from the gross volume. Negative amounts are permitted under Aurora's merchant processing rules but they must appear on a separate reconciliation line item. These were embedded among standard debit entries.

Bottom line: The $14.2M does not represent missing money in the sense of theft or diversion. It represents a complete breakdown in the matching logic between Aurora's receiving engine and Meridian's outgoing declarations. Until Meridian either restores its original reference convention or provides a manual mapping layer, no amount of automated reconciliation can produce a clean set of books between the two organisations. We have recommended a temporary bilateral hold on Meridian-originated settlements until a forensic accounting review is complete.

Conclusion

What we know so far

Finding — F01

Reference conversion was not announced

Meridian's own internal change logs, obtained through a regulatory information request filed under applicable provisions of the Payment Services Directive, confirm that the migration from the MRD- prefix to the new alphanumeric reference scheme was scheduled for 28 July and communicated only to eight named contacts—four of whom had since departed the organisation. No general notification was issued to downstream processors, including Aurora. This omission appears deliberate.

Finding — F02

Timestamp irregularities persist beyond initial exposure

The anomalous timestamps observed on 19 July did not resolve after the alleged "clock-skew correction." Subsequent batches continued to exhibit backward-predating timestamps at a frequency of approximately 0.3% per transaction—roughly eight to twelve instances per batch—through mid-September. The mechanism responsible has not been identified.

Finding — F03

Negative-value transactions suggest possible adjustment manipulation

The three negative-amount transactions embedded within batch ASG-20240822-D06 are particularly significant. If genuine reversals, they belong on a separate line. If fabricated, they constitute a deliberate attempt to reduce the apparent total of the parent batch. Either interpretation requires further examination. Deloitte has designated F03 as a matter of active investigation.

Finding — F04

No evidence of fund loss at the banking level

Despite the matching failure, Aurora's correspondent bank statements confirm that the underlying funds were credited correctly to the respective settlement accounts. The problem is purely informational: we cannot reconcile our internal books against Meridian's declarations because the bridge between the two systems—the reference key—has been removed. The money is accounted for. The paperwork is not.

Finding — F05

Estimated impact window closes at 30 September

Based on the volume patterns observable from clean pre-incident periods, the exposed exposure period—that is, the span of transactions that cannot be matched due to the reference conversion—ends on 30 September 2024. Any Meridian-originated files received after that date will be quarantined automatically by the updated ingestion filter deployed on 2 October. The $14.2M figure is therefore a floor estimate, not a ceiling. Should additional discrepancies emerge during the Deloitte review, the total may rise.
