The Government Content Trust Registry is where verified, cross-referenced policy documents, statistical releases, and public consultations are logged with their chain of custody and provenance metadata. Your first entry will anchor this collection.

  1. 1 Create your first entry. Click "New Entry" above to open the composition form. Provide a title, the issuing department, date, and a plain-language description of the content's purpose.
  2. 2 Assign trust signals. Mark content classification (Public, Official Sensitive, Restricted), attach hash checksums for integrity verification, and set retention windows according to the Government Security Classification Policy.
  3. 3 Publish for discovery. Draft entries remain invisible until you mark them published. Published entries surface in the national open-data catalogue and pass through automated provenance checks against the Government Publishing Authority index.

On drafting entries for the registry

Every entry in this registry becomes part of the permanent audit trail for government publishing activity. Treat each record as if it will be examined by Parliament, a Select Committee, or a future historian. The standards applied here mirror those outlined in the Cabinet Office's Guidance on Transparent Publishing (October 2023) and the Open Data Charter's seven principles.

  • Title fields must be specific and self-describing. Avoid internal acronyms unless they have been defined elsewhere in the registry.
  • The description paragraph should answer: what does this document do, who produced it, and why was it produced? Three to five sentences is typical.
  • Department codes must match the official UK Government organisational taxonomy. Where uncertain, consult the Central Digital and Data Office directory.
  • Date fields use ISO 8601 format (YYYY-MM-DD). If only a month is known, append "-MM". If only a year is known, append "-12-31".
  • Hash checksums (SHA-256) provide tamper evidence. Compute the hash of the final PDF or HTML export; do not hash interim drafts.
  • Tags should reflect subject-matter domains rather than procedural categories. Use "procurement", "environment", "healthcare", "digital infrastructure" — not "Q4-review" or "ministerial-letter".

Classification and handling

Entries may carry sensitivity classifications drawn from the Government Security Classification Framework (GSCF). Do not assign "Official Sensitive" or "Protected" labels without the explicit approval of the relevant Accounting Officer or their delegate. Misclassification can result in both unnecessary restrictions on public access and, in the opposite direction, inadvertent disclosure of controlled material.

Common classification levels
Level Description Retention
Open No restrictions. Suitable for public distribution under the Open Government Licence v3.0. Indefinite
Official Standard business content. Protect against unauthorized access using standard professional precautions. Per schedule or 5 years minimum
Official Sensitive Requires specific safeguards as stated in the handling instruction attached to the record. As specified in classification ruling
Protected Personal data requiring compliance with UK GDPR Article 5 processing principles. Per data protection impact assessment

Provenance and audit expectations

From 2025, all entries entering the registry are expected to carry a machine-readable provenance certificate issued by the Government Publishing Authority. Until the API for automated certificate exchange is operational, authors should manually record the following when creating entries: document reference number, original author, version identifier, and the URL or archive location of the definitive copy.

Entries lacking complete provenance metadata will still be accepted in draft status but will not appear in the published registry or feed to external aggregators until the gaps are filled and a senior reviewer — typically someone with the rank of Grade 6 or equivalent — signs off.
