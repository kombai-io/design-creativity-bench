Interim findings for Evergreen Packaging Ltd following unannounced inspection at Site B, Dunmore Road, Carrickmacross.

Evergreen Packaging Ltd operates a mixed-material reprocessing facility on Dunmore Road in Carrickmacross, County Monaghan. The site has held a combined licence under the European Union (Industrial Emissions) Regulations since 2017, covering the storage, segregation, and onward transmission of post-consumer packaging materials — predominantly corrugated fibreboard, PET bottles, rigid polypropylene, and aluminium cans. The facility processes approximately eighteen thousand tonnes per annum of inbound material.

Marrowfield Environmental was engaged by the Environmental Protection Agency on 3 February 2025 to carry out a targeted compliance inspection following a tip-off received from an anonymous source within the local community. The concern centred on whether waste streams being delivered for recovery were genuinely originating from the declared sources, and whether material previously classified as non-hazardous had been subject to undocumented mixing with regulated fractions during the reporting period spanning Q3 and Q4 of last year.

The inspection team arrived unannounced at 06:40 on Monday 10 March. Upon arrival, they noted that two of the three covered bays designated for segregated storage appeared to contain mixed contents inconsistent with the site's registered waste transfer notes. Furthermore, the electronic weighbridge records showed discrepancies between inbound gross weights and outbound tonnage figures that could not be reconciled through standard loss accounting methods.

Examination of the waste transfer documentation for the period June through December 2024 revealed systematic anomalies in at least fourteen distinct deliveries. Each invoice listed materials as single-stream recovered feedstock — for example, "clean corrugated fibreboard — CAT A" — yet physical sampling confirmed that each batch contained appreciable quantities of other material classes that should have been recorded separately.

The most significant deviation occurred on 12 September 2024, when a lorry bearing registration number W 24-88761 delivered what was documented as 23.4 tonnes of sorted plastic film. Field analysis determined the load actually comprised approximately sixty-two percent plastic film, twenty-eight percent mixed municipal waste including food residue, and ten percent inert construction debris. No accompanying hazardous waste consignment note was produced at the time of delivery.

Delivery Date Declared Actual Composition Deviation
12 Sep 2024 Plastic film — clean 62% film, 28% MSW, 10% inert Critical
03 Oct 2024 PET bottles — rinsed 71% PET, 19% PVC, 10% glass Major
18 Oct 2024 Aluminium cans — compacted 55% Al, 30% steel, 15% ferrous scrap Major
05 Nov 2024 Corrugated board — dry 68% paper, 22% PE-coated, 10% soil Major
22 Nov 2024 Mixed plastics — single-stream 44% PS, 33% PU foam, 23% contaminated Critical
10 Dec 2024 Fibreboard — baled 61% board, 27% biowaste, 12% unknown Critical

In total, six deliveries across four months demonstrated either major or critical misclassification. Three of those six loads were subsequently forwarded to third-party recyclers who operated under the assumption that the material met declared specifications. Those downstream operators — one based in Cork, another in Glasgow, and a smaller unit near Athlone — have been notified and have ceased processing the affected consignments pending further advice from EPA oversight.

The implications extend beyond simple administrative error. When waste carrying undisclosed contaminants reaches a facility designed for clean feedstock, the consequences range from degraded output quality to potential equipment damage and, in the case of biologically active contamination in otherwise inert batches, regulatory breaches under Schedule 3 of the Waste Management Act.

Evidence note:  Photographs taken during the inspection are catalogued under reference IDs PH-4471/01 through PH-4471/24. Physical samples taken from five of the six offending loads are retained at the Marrowfield laboratory in Blanchardstown for confirmatory analysis. Results expected by 18 April 2025.

The remediation plan prepared by Marrowfield Environmental estimates a total programme cost of €347,800 over an estimated twenty-six-week implementation window. The breakdown below outlines each workstream, its responsible party, and the anticipated timing of completion. All figures include contingency at a rate of twelve per cent and assume continued operational status at reduced throughput throughout the works.
