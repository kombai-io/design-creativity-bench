Recipe Overview

Active

The RF-Passive-Array-R4 test recipe was designed for characterizing multi-element surface-mount resistor networks used in high-frequency signal conditioning paths. Each device under test (DUT) contains 8 individually addressable 0402-format resistors arrayed on a single ceramic substrate, requiring sequential probe contact through the L5 loadboard's spring-pin array.

This iteration introduces several refinements over the prior R3 release. Most notably, the S-parameter sweep now covers an extended frequency range down to 100 MHz, capturing parasitic resonances that were previously aliased below our measurement floor. The temperature ramp protocol has also been tightened—each thermal plateau holds for a minimum of 15 minutes to allow full substrate equilibration, reducing measurement drift observed in field returns during automotive-grade qualification programs.

All test vectors have been validated against the specification sheet provided by Murata Manufacturing under part designation LQM21HP series, revision 2.7. Deviations between measured and spec-limited values are within acceptable tolerance margins for production acceptance testing at 50K-unit batch throughput rates.

The thermal cycling profile follows JEDEC JESD22-A104B Level 3 requirements for automotive-grade qualification. Three complete cycles through the −40 °C to 125 °C temperature range, with 15-minute hold periods at each extreme and a ramp rate capped at 15 °C/min to prevent thermal shock-induced delamination of the terminations.

Between each cycle completion, full DC parametric re-characterization is performed to detect resistance drift beyond the ±2.5 % shelf-life spec limit. Devices exhibiting >1.0 % shift during the first cycle are flagged for additional bin-sort analysis.

Functional validation exercises the devices under conditions mimicking real-world circuit operation. An arbitrary waveform generator drives the input nodes with a composite signal containing 10 kHz square waves modulated by a 1 MHz sine carrier, producing realistic transient loading on the internal node capacitance.

The digital multimeter array monitors output node voltages simultaneously across all eight resistive elements, looking for common-mode errors that may indicate solder bridge defects or dielectric breakdown between element pairs. All measurements are time-stamped and logged to the MES database for traceability.
