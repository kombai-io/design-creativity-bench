Your Treatment Journey

How we arrived here

Margaret was admitted to St. Elizabeth Medical Center on March 12 following a transient ischemic event characterized by sudden onset right-sided weakness and expressive aphasia lasting approximately forty minutes. Emergency neurology consulted, CT head negative for hemorrhage, and she was started on aspirin 325 mg daily and high-intensity atorvastatin. Her hospital course included speech-language pathology evaluation, occupational therapy assessment, and inpatient rehabilitation referral.

She was discharged to home care on March 22 with a structured multidisciplinary plan. Physical therapy focused on gait training and balance restoration. Occupational therapy concentrated on activities of daily living, including adaptive techniques for dressing and kitchen tasks. Speech therapy addressed residual language retrieval difficulties and cognitive-communication strategies for workplace re-entry discussions.

Between March and May, her cardiovascular risk profile prompted further investigation. Serial blood work revealed consistently elevated LDL at 148 mg/dL despite maximal statin therapy. Echocardiogram showed preserved ejection fraction at 58% with Grade II diastolic dysfunction. New atrial fibrillation was detected on Holter monitor, leading to warfarin initiation and anticoagulation clinic enrollment. This constellation of findings — the cerebrovascular event, diastolic dysfunction, and new-onset arrhythmia — reframed the clinical picture entirely, shifting the focus from neurorehabilitation alone to integrated cardiometabolic management.

The present plan reflects that evolution. Cardiology has assumed lead coordination, with physical therapy continuing in parallel under the guidance of the rehabilitation medicine physician. Nutritional counseling was added in April following dietitian assessment confirming poor adherence to the Mediterranean dietary pattern prescribed at discharge. The DASH protocol has since been adopted, tailored specifically to accommodate Margaret's food preferences and cultural cooking traditions — because adherence means nothing without practical sustainability.
