Product Recall Advisory — Contamination Alert

Adirondack Foods Inc. has initiated a voluntary Class II recall of select organic snack lines following external laboratory findings of trace Bacillus cereus spores. This document consolidates all verified information for executive review.

On June 9, independent testing commissioned by our quality assurance vendor, LabCorp Environmental Services of Binghamton, detected Bacillus cereus spore counts exceeding regulatory thresholds in three product families sourced from the Malone, New York processing facility. The strains identified are consistent with those found in previously harvested raw materials — primarily dehydrated sweet potato and tapioca starch blends imported from the regional cooperative in Oswego County.

Adirondack Foods operates under a zero-tolerance policy for pathogenic indicators above 10 CFU/g. The samples in question registered between 42 and 187 CFU/g, well above the actionable threshold of 10 and approaching the Class II classification boundary of 200 CFU/g. While no illnesses have been reported to date — and while the detected strains are non-toxigenic, meaning they do not produce emetic or diarrheal toxins — the company's legal team has determined that a precautionary market withdrawal remains the appropriate course of action given the vulnerable consumer base for which these products are marketed: families with young children.

Bottom line: This is a Class II recall. No public health emergencies have been documented. No patient hospitalizations have occurred. Our internal medical consultant, Dr. Priya Menon of Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute, considers the acute risk profile "minimal but non-zero." The decision to recall reflects both regulatory prudence and brand stewardship. Estimated financial exposure sits at approximately $3.8M when accounting for inventory write-down, logistics, and customer remediation costs.

Action Chronology

Key milestones in the detection-to-response chain. Each entry represents a finalized action point verified by the response operations team.

  1. June 4–8, 2025
    Sampling Campaign
    Adirondack Foods QA staff, accompanied by a LabCorp observer, collected 23 samples from finished goods inventory at the Malone facility and three storage silos in Oswego County. Samples held at 4 °C pending transport to LabCorp's Binghamton lab.
  2. June 9, Morning
    Alert Generated
    LabCorp flagged elevated B. cereus counts in three lots. Immediate notification sent to QA Director Tomasz Kowalski and Medical Consultant Dr. Priya Menon. Product hold orders issued across the affected SKUs.
  3. June 9, Afternoon
    Internal Escalation
    Crisis Response Protocol Level 3 activated. Marcus Bellweather convened initial response team call with Elena Vasquez, Sarah Okonkwo, and Tomasz Kowalski. Preliminary impact assessment begun.
  4. June 10–11
    Regulatory Engagement & Supply Chain Mapping
    FDA pre-notification conducted. Traceability exercise completed: all inbound raw materials from the Oswego cooperative quarantined. Outbound distribution mapped across 31-state network. Third-party verification audit initiated by Deloitte Food Safety Practice.
  5. June 12, 09:00
    Public Release
    Press release distributed via Business Wire. Consumer FAQ page launched. Social media channels updated. Direct customer notifications dispatched to loyalty program members (estimated 14,000 contacts) whose purchase history matched recalled lot numbers.
  6. June 13 (Scheduled)
    Board Briefing
    Emergency board session convened. Full financial impact analysis to be presented. Legal counsel to provide liability assessment. Strategic options discussed regarding temporary production halts at the Malone facility pending root cause identification.

Root cause investigation is ongoing. The leading hypothesis, articulated by food safety microbiologist Dr. Kenji Watanabe of Cornell University's College of Agriculture and Life Sciences — who is serving as an independent consultant engaged by Adirondack Foods' legal team — points to a failure in the thermal drying process used on the dehydrated sweet potato puree sheets received from the Oswego cooperative. If confirmed, this would indicate a supply-chain quality failure rather than a manufacturing process breakdown. The distinction carries important implications for warranty claims against the raw-material supplier and for the structural changes needed in incoming inspection protocols.
