I.

Background & History

Catherine Whitfield was received into the curacy of St Jude’s Parish, Deptford, in the autumn of 2019. A graduate of Cuddesdon College and later of King’s College London, where she earned her master’s degree in practical theology, she entered the parish with a clear conviction about urban ministry — specifically, the intersection of community development and sacramental life in post-industrial neighbourhoods.

During her five years at St Jude’s, Rev. Whitfield expanded the parish’s outreach programme substantially. She founded the neighbourhood food partnership, secured multi-year funding from the Archbishop’s Fund for Mission, and oversaw the conversion of the former school hall into a community workspace now used weekly by thirty-two local organisations. Her annual reports, consistently well above expectation, documented measurable increases in congregational engagement, youth participation, and partnerships with neighbouring parishes across the Borough of Lewisham.

“Catherine possesses a rare temperament — equal parts strategic clarity and genuine hospitality. Those who serve alongside her describe an approachability that disarms even the most hesitant participants.” — The Venerable Diana Marsh, Archdeacon of Southwark

The trajectory of her ministry, however, shifted significantly during the winter of 2023. Following the sudden departure of the parish priest, Rev. Whitfield assumed interim pastoral responsibility for a congregation already grieving a leadership transition. While she managed the immediate demands competently, contemporaneous wellbeing assessments completed in March 2024 noted early signs of chronic fatigue, declining sleep quality, and emotional depletion — patterns consistent with sustained caregiving without adequate restorative structures.

II.

Current Placement & Service Record

As of June 2024, Reverend Whitfield holds the position of Honorary Assistant in the Diocese of Southwark, attached to All Saints’ Church, Wandsworth. This arrangement was ratified by the Bishop on 1 July 2024, following completion of the tripartite consultation involving the incumbent, the archdeaconry, and the diocesan wellbeing panel.

The current appointment carries several deliberate modifications designed to reduce cumulative stress while maintaining meaningful ministerial engagement. Hours are capped at twenty per week — approximately forty per cent below the average full-time curacy load recorded over the preceding eighteen months. Preaching responsibilities are shared on a rotating basis among two other incumbents, and no pastoral visitation burden exceeds one per day without prior consent.

III.

Way Forward & Recommendations

The path ahead for Reverend Whitfield has been constructed with sensitivity to both her demonstrated gifts and her recognised limits. The intention is neither to diminish her vocation nor to shield her from challenge entirely, but rather to create the conditions under which her capacity can recover fully before the next phase of responsibility begins.

Restorative Sabbatical — Autumn 2024

A six-week leave period is recommended, commencing 9 September and concluding 20 October 2024. During this interval, Rev. Whitfield is encouraged to distance herself from ministry environments altogether — spending extended periods in the Lake District, revisiting monastic communities she visited during seminary studies, and engaging in reflective reading unconnected to any professional outcome. Full salary continues throughout. A return meeting will be scheduled for the first week of November with the Archdeacon and the Diocesan Officer for Clergy Care.

Expanded Role from Spring 2025

Following successful completion of the sabbatical and confirmation of sustained wellbeing improvement, it is anticipated that Rev. Whitfield will be considered for a part-time curacy or stipended role equivalent to fifty-five per cent of a standard FTE. Preferred placements identified include Christ Church, Peckham — where the team is seeking someone with community development expertise — or an educational ministry appointment at St Paul’s Cathedral, overseeing the junior lectionary syllabus currently managed by a contracted external provider.

Ongoing Supervision & Support

Twice-monthly supervisory meetings with a designated clinical supervisor (to be appointed by the Diocese’s Clergy Support Unit) are advised for the twelve months following her return to work. Additionally, Rev. Whitfield has been invited to join the Diocesan Leadership Cohort, a peer group of six senior clergy that meets quarterly for mutual reflection and formational practice — a setting chosen specifically for its balance of camaraderie and gentle accountability.

“God does not call us to burn out; He calls us to bear fruit — and fruit grows in season, not through constant tearing down of the vine.” — From the discharge summary, Rev. Whitfield’s personal journal, April 2024

The placement plan described in this document has been endorsed by the Bishop of Southwark, the Venerable Diana Marsh (Archdeacon), Rev. Dr James Hartwell (Incumbent, All Saints’), and Rev. Whitfield herself. All parties affirm their commitment to the principles outlined above and agree to reconvene formally every quarter to assess progress, adjust provisions as necessary, and celebrate the continued faithful service being rendered — albeit at a measured and sustainable pace — by a gifted member of the ordained ministry whose resilience remains undiminished, even where her energy has faltered.
